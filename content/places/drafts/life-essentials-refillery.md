## How a refill shop usually works, without adding rules this one did not print

This is general advice for a bulk grocery that invites you to bring a jar. It is not a house policy copied from Life Essentials, because the site does not print container rules, a tare weight, or a sanitation step.

Ask whether your jar is accepted, and whether the price is by weight. The homepage says to bring a jar for spices and teas. It does not say every bulk food, including grains or honey, uses the same jar. Ask which items are refill-only and which are sold prepacked. The copper bottle, the mug, the bracelet, and the napkins are finished goods with printed prices, not loose herbs.

Ask what “responsibly sourced in Tampa” means for the meat or dairy you want that week. The homepage says meats and dairy are responsibly sourced in Tampa. It does not name the farms on that page. If a farm name matters to you, ask Sheila’s team for it.

Ask about pets, home care, and personal care as separate aisles. The navigation lists all three. A cleaning product and a tea are not interchangeable just because they sit on one site.

If you want a class, a knife sharpened, or a Vinted drop-off, call first. The labels exist. The schedule does not, on the pages we read.
