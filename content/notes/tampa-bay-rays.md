# Tampa Bay Rays Guide: Tropicana Field Home Again

Search “Tampa Rays” and half the pins still aim at downtown Tampa. The club is the **Tampa Bay Rays**. The home ballpark is **Tropicana Field** at 1 Tropicana Drive, St. Petersburg, FL 33705. The team’s own line is “Back In the Burg.” If the word Tampa alone sends you across the wrong bridge, you will miss first pitch.

This guide covers the 2026 return to the Trop after the hurricane year, how tickets and bags work, and how Steinbrenner Field fits the story without becoming today’s address. **Last updated:** November 2, 2026.

## Visit Snapshot

- **Team:** Tampa Bay Rays (American League East)
- **Home ballpark (2026):** Tropicana Field, 1 Tropicana Dr, St. Petersburg, FL 33705
- **2025 temporary home:** George M. Steinbrenner Field, Tampa (Yankees spring complex)—not the current regular-season home
- **Spring training:** Charlotte Sports Park, Port Charlotte
- **Tickets:** Mobile only via MLB Ballpark app
- **Bags:** Clear-bag policy (see details below)
- **Cash:** Cash-free ballpark (reverse ATMs available)
- **Child tickets:** Required from a child’s second birthday (under 2 as lap child)

Confirm security and parking on the Rays’ [ballpark guide](https://www.mlb.com/rays/ballpark/information/guide) and [security page](https://www.mlb.com/rays/ballpark/information/security).

## Why “Tampa” Still Confuses People

For one season the home games really were in Tampa. After Hurricane Helene flooded the Tropicana Field area and Hurricane Milton tore the roof apart in October 2024, the club spent 2025 outdoors at Steinbrenner Field while the City of St. Petersburg—owner of the ballpark—repaired the roof. Club materials put the repair near $60 million (another figure cited $59.7 million); I leave both. The last roof panel went in November 21, 2025. AECOM Hunt, which the club says helped build the Florida Suncoast Dome in 1990, ran the repair. Fabric panels came as PTFE-coated fiberglass cut into 24 panels, about 5,000 pounds each, installed more than 200 feet up.

The return opener against the Cubs was Monday, April 6, 2026, 4:10 p.m. ET—first game in the building in 561 days by the club’s count. By October 2026 the Rays were playing October baseball under that roof again. The temporary Tampa address is history for regular-season planning; the living address is St. Petersburg.

Longer term, the club still talks about a forever home and has floated a proposed Tampa plan. Later 2026 accounts described Hillsborough / Tampa votes toward a financed stadium concept near Hillsborough College’s Dale Mabry campus with a 2029 target—bond validation and groundbreaking still ahead when those stories ran. Nothing in those accounts says a replacement park is open now. Through at least the end of the 2028 Trop lease window described in those reports, treat Tropicana Field as home.

Devil Rays history starts with an inaugural game March 31, 1998. The name used today is Tampa Bay Rays. The building St. Petersburg owns is still the one with the gate you need.

## Getting Into the Trop

Tickets live in the MLB Ballpark app—mobile only. Gate box offices at Gates 1 and 4 help with mobile tickets on game days starting 90 minutes before first pitch; you can scan a QR code to buy. It is not a paper-ticket window. Gates open 90 minutes before first pitch Monday–Friday and two hours before on Opening Day and weekend home games (confirm postseason listings—they sometimes publish their own gate clocks). All gates are wheelchair accessible. VIP Gate 4 serves suites and named premium rooms (DEX Home Plate Club, The Baldwin Group Club, Two Rivers Home Plate Box, Chalk Box, Baseline Premier). Screening uses an Evolv walk-through.

**Clear bags:** one single-compartment clear bag (plastic, vinyl, or PVC) no larger than **12″ × 12″ × 6″** (security pages also show **12″ × 6″ × 12″**—same volume, different axis order; measure before you leave). Plus one non-clear clutch or fanny pack ≤ **5″ × 7″**. Backpacks stay out. Diaper bags with a young child and medical-device bags are the named exceptions. No bag check—wrong bag means a walk back to the car.

**Cash-free:** credit, debit, Rays Wallet. Republic Bank ATM in the Republic Bank Draft Room; reverse ATMs across from Gate 3 guest services and in the 3rd Base Food Hall. Alcohol: 21+, two drinks per person per purchase, none in or out, sales stop mid-8th. Outside water: one sealed bottle—guest code language also allows 20 oz or less, or one empty plastic bottle/cup per ticketed fan.

First aid (Orlando Health St. Petersburg) sits between Gates 3 and 4 on the 100 level. Elevators at Gates 1, 2, 4, and 6. MaintenX SkyDeck above left field is all-inclusive food, beer, wine, and soft drinks; one club update quoted tickets starting at $49—reconfirm before you buy. Cownose Clubhouse: about 35 feet / 10,000 gallons beyond right-center; the seven stingrays that rode out Milton are back. Longoria and Iwamura statues returned at Gate 4. Netting runs home to foul poles at sections 137–138. Group sales: 727-825-3406.

## Parking and Getting Across From Tampa

Team parking guidance shifts with the calendar. In postseason mode the club has required **prepaid-only** lots—no drive-up—and sent overflow to downtown St. Petersburg lots it does not control. Lot 1 is premium (handy for seats behind home plate). Lots 6 and 7 are general. Wheelchair spaces: Lots 1 and 7 (mostly 7), van-accessible, entered from 3rd or 4th Avenue South; police watch placard use. Drop-off in front of Gate 4 across from Lot 1 on 16th Street South. Rideshare: Gate 4 generally; ADA rideshare Gate 1 or 4. Oversized vehicles may be redirected.

**SunRunner** BRT stops on the north side of the field—every 15 minutes 6 a.m.–8 p.m., every 30 minutes 8 p.m.–midnight. The fan guide also mentions a Looper baseball shuttle in 2026 (one older line still said 2024)—treat trolley details as something to confirm, not memorize. From Tampa, the bay crossing is its own plan: see [Tampa to St. Petersburg](/guides/tampa-to-st-petersburg). Do not aim for Raymond James Stadium on Dale Mabry unless you meant football—see [Tampa Buccaneers](/events/tampa-buccaneers).

## What “Under the Roof” Fixed

Besides the fabric, the club described a new field: three-quarter-inch pad on concrete, more than 137,000 square feet of Shaw Sports Turf, 450 tons of DuraEdge clay, and a walnut-shell/sand warning track between bullpens. New ownership funded extras beyond the city repair—premium pockets and an expanded video board among them. Players quoted by the club missed the air-conditioning; one line put the building at 72 degrees and noted zero home rainouts at Steinbrenner—a compliment to the temporary park and a reason they wanted the dome back.

## Premium Pockets and What Changed Indoors

Club updates after the roof work describe new premium seating pockets: a Home Plate Box, Baseline Premier behind the Rays bullpen, and a Chalk Box on the first baseline, plus the MaintenX SkyDeck all-inclusive perch above left field. Those are ticket products, not walk-up lounge names you invent at the gate. If your confirmation lists one of those clubs, use VIP Gate 4 guidance and arrive with time to clear screening.

The field under the new roof is not the old carpet story fans tell from earlier decades. The Under the Roof materials describe a pad on concrete, Shaw Sports Turf measured in six figures of square feet, hundreds of tons of infield clay, and a warning track mixed from walnut shell and sand running bullpen to bullpen. You do not need those numbers to enjoy a game. You do need them if someone insists “they never fixed anything but the roof.”

## Tips for a Tampa-Based Fan Night

- Say **St. Petersburg** out loud when you share the plan.
- Buy mobile tickets and prepaid parking early for big nights.
- Pack a **clear** bag—opposite of Raymond James’ no-bag clutch rule.
- Budget bridge time; FDOT’s city pair is 20 miles, not “next door.”
- Ignore leftover Steinbrenner “see a Rays game in Tampa” pages when you are buying a 2026 Trop date.
- Keep football and hockey on separate nights unless you enjoy sitting in I-275 traffic between Dale Mabry and Channelside.
- If you are combining a morning in Tampa with a 7:00 p.m. first pitch, eat before you cross—or build a St. Petersburg dinner plan near the park.

## FAQ

### Are the Tampa Rays in Tampa?

The team is the Tampa Bay Rays. Home games in 2026 are at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg. The 2025 Steinbrenner Field season was the hurricane temporary home.

### What is the Tropicana Field address?

1 Tropicana Drive (also printed as 1 Tropicana Field Drive), St. Petersburg, FL 33705.

### Do I need a clear bag?

Yes. One clear single-compartment bag within the posted size limit, plus a small clutch/fanny pack up to 5″ × 7″. No bag storage for non-compliant bags.

### Are tickets paper?

No. Mobile only through the MLB Ballpark app.

### Is there a new Rays stadium open now?

No. Tropicana Field is the active home. Proposed Tampa plans described in 2026 reporting were not an open ballpark you can drive to for tonight’s game.

## Mixing a Trop Night With Tampa Plans

A clean pattern when you are based downtown or in South Tampa: morning errands or a museum on the Tampa side, early afternoon bridge crossing, early arrival for parking and the clear-bag check, then baseball. Trying to squeeze a Buccaneers kickoff and a Rays evening into one day usually fails on traffic alone. Lightning hockey on Channelside is closer to downtown Tampa lodging than Tropicana Field is—still not a substitute for reading the venue column.

If someone in your group still argues “the Rays play in Tampa,” show them the 2025 Steinbrenner exception as the reason the argument exists—then show the 2026 Tropicana address as the reason the argument ends.
