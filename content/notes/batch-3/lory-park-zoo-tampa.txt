People say "lory park zoo tampa" all the time, and I want to be clear about what I found. There is no Lory Park Zoo. I looked for a separate park, a bird garden, a different address. Nothing. The place people are trying to reach is ZooTampa at Lowry Park, 1101 W. Sligh Avenue, Tampa, FL 33604. Lowry, with a w. Not lory. The zoo's phone is (813) 935-8552.

The misspelling is sticky for two reasons I actually saw. First, Lowry and Lory sound the same if you have only heard the name. Second, the real zoo does have lorikeets. Lorikeet Landing sits in the Asia realm, a free-flight aviary, and the zoo says you can feed them for a small fee. I am not going to invent that fee. The point is that a person who hears "lory" can see a lorikeet photo and think they found a different attraction called Lory Park. They did not. It is one zoo, and the lorikeets are one aviary inside it.

The naming history is worth knowing, because the signs and the legal name do not match the way people talk. It started in the 1930s as a city department with a few Florida animals. In 1982 community leaders set up the Lowry Park Zoo Association to run it for the city. That became the Lowry Park Zoological Society of Tampa, Inc., which is still the nonprofit. A rebuilt 24-acre zoo first welcomed guests in 1988 and got Association of Zoos and Aquariums accreditation. The zoo later says the place has grown to 56 acres of naturalistic exhibits. Separately, the zoo says it sits inside a 67-acre natural park that also has wild Florida animals the zoo does not control. I am writing all three numbers down because squashing them into one "the zoo is X acres" is how bad directions start.

They say they care for more than 1,000 animals and take part in nearly 100 AZA Species Survival Programs.

What the visit is actually like, from walking it realm by realm. The realms they organize around: Manatee Circle at the front, then Asia, Florida, Carousel Circle, Primates, Australia, Africa. Manatee Circle has the Canopy Aviary, the Macaw Flyover, fountains, Macaw's Landing for food, and Nature's Kingdom gift shop.

Asia has Malayan tigers, sun bears, greater one-horned rhinos, plus that lorikeet aviary and a Sulawesi aviary on the boardwalk.

Florida is the one I would not skip: panthers, black bears, alligators, red wolves, flamingos, owls, and the manatee work. The zoo describes the David A. Straz, Jr. Manatee Critical Care Center as the first nonprofit acute-care facility of its kind for wild manatees and one of two federally permitted manatee rehab centers in the U.S. They say the team has treated over 600 manatees since 1991, most released. Florida Waters is the new expansion: Manatee Rescue with underwater viewing and jellies, Otter Oasis, Reptile and Amphibian Discovery, and Stingray Shores still there. Roaring Springs is the boat ride to a three-story splashdown. Height minimum 36 inches, and kids 36 to 42 inches need someone 14 or older.

Africa is elephants, giraffes, white rhinos, shoebills, painted dogs, okapi, pygmy hippos. Expedition Wild Africa is included in admission. So are the other rides, the zoo says. Encounters are not. Giraffe, tortoise, rhino, penguin, koala behind the scenes, manatee tour: extra, reserved, limited. The zoo is blunt that touching is not guaranteed. Penguins, southern white rhinos, and giant tortoises only if the animal is comfortable and staff allow it. Everything else, no touching. Lorikeets and stingrays may touch you. Do not feed anything that is not part of a paid feeding. Animals are on set diets.

Australia is the cool-off zone: Billabong splash pad, Aussie Trek, Boomer's Flyin' Bananas for small kids (30 to 48 inches, adults cannot ride), Tasmanian Tiger Coaster (36 inches, under 48 inches with someone 14+), koala clubhouse. The carousel is included. Adults cannot ride the animals. Kids need an adult or a companion 14 or older. The primates are mostly endangered species.

Hours, and I am separating the standing schedule from a one-day notice. The standard hours the zoo gives are 9:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., seven days a week. Closed Thanksgiving. Christmas Eve 9:30 to 4. Closed Christmas Day. Special event days can differ. On the day I checked, the zoo was also announcing Saturday Creatures of the Night, 9:30 a.m. to 10:00 p.m., while its regular hours still said open today 9:30 to 5. I would not freeze either of those into "always." Check the day.

The zoo was also still posting a notice that it would close at 2 p.m. on Thursday, September 18, for an AZA conference, last entry 1 p.m. That date was already past when I was there in October. Leftover notices are how people turn around in the parking lot. A current notice I would believe: construction on the front gate arrival plaza, temporary entrance and exit. If your old memory of the front door does not match, that is why.

Tickets, straight from the zoo, not from a third-party seller. General admission $51.95 adults 12 and up, $41.95 children 3 to 11. Buying the single-day ticket ahead of time saves $3 each. Reservations are not required for a normal visit. They are required for Signature Encounters, and those sell out, so buy ahead if you care. A printed ticket is unnecessary if the barcode is on your phone, in a wallet app, or in their app. If the confirmation email never comes, check spam for "ZooTampa," or go to the window with the order number or a photo ID and your name.

Zoo Fun tickets are not memberships. The zoo told me a Zoo Fun ticket gets repeat admission through the end of 2027, plus Creatures of the Night and Christmas in the Wild except on blockout dates. 2026 blockouts: October 10, 17, and 24 after 4 p.m. during Creatures of the Night, and November 27 and 28 including Christmas in the Wild. 2027: March 20 through 28, October 9, 16, 23, and 30 after 4 p.m., and November 26 and 27. Blockouts hit repeat visits. The first use of a Zoo Fun ticket can be on a blockout day. Memberships are the thing with free parking, no blockout dates, guest privileges, dining discounts, and reciprocal admission. Pair $134, Trio $194, Quad $244, Family $294, with unlimited admission through the end of 2027. I am copying their prices, not promising they stay. They also say Zoo Fun and memberships are easy to confuse, and I believe them, because I confused them.

Parking is the other fight. The zoo says $10 a day through ParkMobile for non-members, including Zoo Fun holders. Members park free. The figure I was given was $10. I am not going to quote an older figure I could not confirm. Register member vehicles or you can still get a parking ticket. That is the kind of rule people miss.

Food rules are stricter than a picnic zoo. Restaurants inside. Outside food is for dietary needs: sealed water up to 20 ounces, one per guest, small snacks for young kids, baby food, a tiny soft cooler per child, medical food. No prepared meals, no alcohol, no glass, no big coolers, no wheeled suitcases over the size they set. The picnic area is outside, near the main lot, and alcohol is not allowed there either. If you packed lunch, you eat it before you go through the gate or you go back out. Bags get inspected. No drones over the campus at all. No plastic bags, straws, or cup lids, for the animals. Smoking and vaping only in designated spots, and no marijuana. Only ADA service animals, including service animals in training. Not pets, not emotional support animals. No pet boarding. Service animals can be restricted from a habitat that day, and they are kept out of the koala photo encounter. Wheelchairs: the zoo says it is accessible, with some steeper grades. Rentals at entry or Nature's Kingdom. Refundable $25 card hold. Manual wheelchair $15, stroller $16 or $15 for members, ECV $42, first come, 18 and up to drive, 450 pound limit, one person, no reservations. Open rain or shine except extreme weather.

Discounts people expect and the zoo says no. Military and veterans: $4 off per ticket, up to four general admissions, ID, bought at the gate. Seniors: $2 off at the window. They explicitly do not discount Florida residents, AARP, EBT, AAA, teachers, or students. No free birthday admission. Groups of 15 or more are handled separately through group sales.

The wildlife warning is not boilerplate. Wild animals live in that park outside the collection. Do not approach, touch, or feed them. Stay back from ponds and tall grass because of alligators and snakes. They also warn about honeybees in the outdoor areas and say they manage them rather than wipe them out.

So: lory park zoo tampa is a wrong spelling for ZooTampa at Lowry Park. The lorikeets are real. The separate zoo is not. Go to Sligh Avenue, watch for the temporary front gate, find out whether today is a normal 9:30 to 5 or an event night, and do not show up with a cooler and a printed map that says Lory.
