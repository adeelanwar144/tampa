Notes on ocean prime tampa

Ocean Prime Tampa, Ocean Prime Tampa FL, and Ocean Prime in Tampa Florida are the same restaurant. I checked because the name gets said three ways and I half-expected a downtown room and a Westshore room. There is one Tampa Ocean Prime. It sits at the entrance to International Plaza, on the corner of West Shore Boulevard and Boy Scout Boulevard. The address they give: International Plaza, 2205 N. Westshore Boulevard, Tampa, FL 33607. Phone (813) 490-5288. Visit Tampa Bay writes the street as 2205 N Westshore Blvd with the same phone. Some reservation systems split it as West Shore. Same building. It is not on the Riverwalk, not in Hyde Park, and not a second concept hiding under a slightly different name.

Cameron Mitchell Restaurants. The restaurant says the company story starts in 1993 and that Ocean Prime has passed 20 locations from Beverly Hills to New York. Executive Chef Jason Mallon, General Manager Jonathan Pollock. Steak and seafood together, a sushi list inside that, a lounge, California and Italian bottles. Not a trattoria and not a Thai grill. Visit Tampa Bay files it as fine dining.

Hours I would actually plan around

The restaurant's own hours are the clock I trusted. Lunch Monday through Friday, 12 to 4 p.m. Dinner Monday through Friday, 4 to 10 p.m. Saturday 3 to 10 p.m. Sunday 5 to 9 p.m. Visit Tampa Bay compresses the weekday into one span, Monday through Friday 12 p.m. to 10 p.m., Saturday 3 to 10, Sunday 5 to 9, which matches if you stack lunch against dinner. Where I noticed people get stuck is Saturday and Sunday. There is no Saturday lunch service. The room opens at 3 p.m. on Saturday and at 5 p.m. on Sunday. A mall Saturday does not mean the restaurant is serving at noon just because International Plaza is open.

Happy hour is narrower than people hope. Monday through Friday, lounge only, 4 to 6 p.m., $5 off the lounge menu plus specially priced cocktails, beer, and wine. It is not the whole dining room and it is not a two-hour extension after 6. The restaurant says they do not offer a weekend brunch. Holiday brunch happens sometimes; they tell you to join the VIPrime list for those dates rather than assuming Mother's Day or Easter is announced somewhere else. If someone promises Saturday brunch at this Tampa Ocean Prime, I would not believe it without the restaurant saying so.

Reservations

Reservations are recommended, not required. If no times show up when you try to book, the restaurant says call anyway. Parties of 5 or more need a credit card. A 20 percent gratuity is customarily added at 6 or more. Those are different thresholds and people mix them up. I would not walk in at 7 p.m. on a Friday and hope. Call (813) 490-5288 if the reservation system looks closed. Outside wine is allowed. Corkage on the menu I saw was $35 a bottle.

Attire, and the Las Vegas rule that is not Tampa

The Tampa restaurant calls the atmosphere upscale and elegant, a lunch, dinner, and happy hour place. The company's dress guidelines ask for polished clothes suited to the restaurant. They do not want clothing that throws off a heavy marijuana smell, excessively revealing clothes, exposed undergarments, or shirts with offensive language or graphics. Management can refuse service. Then the same guidelines have a Las Vegas section: no men's sleeveless shirts, no swimwear-looking outfits, no ballcaps in the main dining room. That section is labeled Las Vegas. I thought people were struggling because they took the Vegas rules and decided a Tampa man in a polo without a jacket, or a guest in a plain cap, was automatically out. The Tampa restaurant does not carry over those three bans. Polished still means polished. Beach cover-ups and gym shirts are the mismatch, not a missing necktie. I would still dress for a steakhouse at a mall entrance, not for the food court.

Parking at International Plaza

Valet service is available. That is the restaurant's answer, full stop. They do not give a valet price, so I am not writing one down. Visit Tampa Bay says both self park and valet. The restaurant's own description of the building puts you at the entrance of the shopping center, corner of Westshore and Boy Scout, not deep inside a department store. The stuck moment is treating the whole International Plaza garage system as if every space were the restaurant's. Self-parking for this room is the parking in front of the restaurant, not a lap through the mall because a store entrance looked closer. If you are coming for a 6 p.m. reservation, give yourself mall-traffic time on Westshore. The restaurant cannot fix Boy Scout Boulevard.

What the menu is built to do

I went through the Tampa menus and I am not going to pretend those prices are frozen. The restaurant says pricing and menu items change. The shape of the menu is what matters.

The raw bar is shrimp cocktail, oysters, chilled crab, a dressed lobster tail, Dutch Harbor king crab at market, and a custom "smoking" shellfish tower also at market. Rose & Garnet is their private-label Cape Cod oyster with Cape Cod Oyster Co. Starters I noted: caviar deviled eggs, parmesan baked scallops, jumbo lump crab cake, ahi tuna tartare, king crab beef carpaccio.

Sushi sits on the steakhouse menu. Prime roll, ocean roll, spicy tuna, hamachi crudo, a nigiri flight. It is not a separate sushi counter and I did not see a Tampa omakase. Dinner seafood included blackened snapper, Chilean sea bass, lobster linguine, miso black cod, branzino, scallops. Chops beyond steak: roasted chicken, Saddleberk Farms pork, rack of lamb. Prime steaks are broiled at 1200 degrees: filets including Piedmontese and Australian wagyu, New York strip, a dry-aged strip, ribeye, and a 22 oz bone-in ribeye. Finishes cost extra. Béarnaise, truffle butter, Oscar style, garlic shrimp. Sides are separate too: jalapeño au gratin, truffle mac and cheese, tater tots, broccolini. A steak dinner here is steak plus finish plus side. I noticed people arguing because they thought the filet price was the plate.

Weekday lunch from noon has a two-course prix fixe, soup or salad plus something like short rib dip, shrimp linguine, snapper, or steak frites, plus a lobster roll and a wagyu cheeseburger. Happy hour food is the shorter lounge menu: sliders, fish tacos, broiled oysters. Not the 22 oz ribeye trick.

Desserts when I looked: ten layer carrot cake, chocolate peanut butter pie, crème brûlée, warm butter cake. The wine list is long. The restaurant names Ruinart, Far Niente, Caymus, DAOU, Paul Hobbs, and Orin Swift, some of it by Coravin, with Italian bottles called out. That is a wine focus, not an Italian restaurant.

Gluten-free friendly items are marked GFM, meaning the dish is prepared that way or can be modified. The kitchen is not gluten-free. The menu names the major allergens in the building: peanuts, tree nuts, eggs, fish, shellfish, milk, soy, and wheat. Tell the server. A marked item is not a sealed promise.

Private dining, and which capacity number to trust

I used the restaurant's own room chart. Westshore and Bayshore, 25 seated and 30 standing each, and they combine. Prime Room, 30 seated and 45 standing. Board Room, 15 seated, with a projector. Formal Terrace, 100 seated and 140 standing, 2,200 square feet. East Corner Terrace, 20 seated and 30 standing. Partial and full buyouts exist. Events go through (813) 490-5288.

Visit Tampa Bay is close but sloppier: it says the Prime room holds 35, puts indoor seats at 291 and total capacity at 423, and in one place caps the largest banquet at 50 even while saying the Formal Terrace holds 140 for a reception. When they disagree, I follow the restaurant's room chart. The terrace is seasonal and only partly enclosed. I would not promise a patio table in a month I have not asked about. They will arrange event valet, flowers, AV, and cakes. Ordering ahead for takeout exists for people who want the food without the room. I did not test how far delivery runs.

Where the night goes wrong

The struggles I kept coming back to were all planning, not the crab cake. One restaurant, at International Plaza, not a downtown twin. Saturday opens at 3, Sunday at 5, no regular weekend brunch. Happy hour is the lounge, 4 to 6, weekdays. Five guests need a card, six guests get a 20 percent gratuity, and those are different thresholds. Valet exists, self-parking is in front of the restaurant, and the mall garages are a different problem. The Las Vegas hat-and-sleeveless rules are in the company dress guidelines and are not the Tampa rules. Steak prices are not plate prices once finishes and sides land. GFM is not a gluten-free kitchen. If OpenTable looks full, the restaurant itself says to call.

Whether people say ocean prime tampa, ocean prime tampa fl, or ocean prime in tampa florida, that Westshore address is the whole answer. Dress like you meant to go to a steakhouse, book the terrace only if the season is actually on, and do not build a Saturday lunch around a room that is not serving yet.
