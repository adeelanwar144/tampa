Notes on olivia tampa

I had to settle which restaurant "olivia tampa" is, because the apostrophe version, "olivia's tampa," sounds like a different cafe, and because Chris Ponte already had a well-known place on Swann Avenue before this one. It is one brand. The restaurant styles itself OLIVIA, no apostrophe. People who say it with the apostrophe still mean the same South Tampa Italian restaurant; I could not find a second Olivia's diner at another address. The Tampa location is 3601 W. Swann Avenue, Tampa, FL 33609, phone 813-328-8866. That is the room the name means.

There is a second OLIVIA, and it is not in Tampa. St. Petersburg, 211 1st Avenue N, phone 727-746-9240. That room had been scheduled for January 21, 2025, and as of November 2025 that was still the date going around. OLIVIA now says it is up and running. If the confirmation says 1st Avenue, you are in St. Pete.

The other Swann mistake is On Swann. Contemporary American, debuted May 29, 2016, in Hyde Park Village, 1501 W. Swann Avenue, phone 813-251-0110. Ponte is executive chef and partner there too. It runs lunch and a weekend morning service from 10 a.m. OLIVIA is the later Italian room, farther west, in the old Carmel Kitchen space. Cafe Ponte is earlier still and is not this building. "The Ponte place on Swann" is how people mix them. For olivia tampa the address is 3601.

What they say the restaurant is

They call it modern Italian in Tampa and St. Pete: trattoria flavors, a chef-driven menu, casual sophistication, craft cocktails. The working dough room is supposed to be visible, pizza dough, mozzarella, pasta, and ravioli. Full bar, lounge, a temperature-controlled wine wall, a sommelier for American and Italian bottles. Northern and southern Italian cooking, named for the youngest child. Ponte studied at Le Cordon Bleu in Paris and started with Cafe Ponte. The restaurant credits Cafe Ponte with a national dining guide's "Best restaurant on the Gulf Coast" and an industry Hall of Fame induction. The Ponte Group is the family company with his wife Michelle and son JT Mahoney. They also say the team is led by head chef Benjamin Clark. Guests ask for Ponte. The kitchen lead they name now is Clark.

OLIVIA dates to February 2019, in the old Carmel Kitchen, with Empad's white brick and black and brass. Come-as-you-are was the family's line. It is a Michelin Guide selection: Michelin has it as Italian and American, $$$, at 3601 W. Swann. That is not a star. I would not call it a starred restaurant. The name is Ponte's daughter, pastas are extruded and dried in house, and the cooking is classic without being by-the-book.

Hours, and why I do not want to guess brunch

The restaurant itself did not give me a weekly schedule. Their "Now open for dinner!" line is a slogan, not a schedule. The hours I was given were Monday through Thursday 5 p.m. to 9 p.m., Friday 5 p.m. to 10 p.m., Saturday 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., Sunday 10 a.m. to 9 p.m., and I got the same pattern a second time. I am using that as the hour set I actually have, and I would still call 813-328-8866 before I promised someone a Saturday morning table, because the restaurant was not confirming that schedule itself. Saturday and Sunday starting at 10 a.m. is the part that does not match a dinner-only mental model. I am not calling it brunch. The restaurant never used that word with me. On Swann, the other Ponte room, does run a weekend morning service, and I think some of the confusion leaks from there. If you need lunch on a Wednesday, OLIVIA's hours do not give it to you. You are looking at 5 p.m.

Reservations and parking

Parties of 7 or more have to call 813-328-8866. The restaurant is clear about that. Smaller tables can book ahead without calling. There is a March 2020 account that is stale on prices and still useful on the room: a diner with a reservation was pointed at the bar and sat about 20 minutes late while tables were rebuilt for bigger parties. I am not treating that as this week's wait. I am treating it as the failure mode. A busy room will move furniture for an eight-top, and a pair watches. Seven or more, use the phone.

Parking, per the restaurant: valet directly in front of the restaurant, and complimentary parking around the building, first come, first served. Valet and a car park are noted by Michelin as well, along with no pets allowed. I did not find a valet price, so I am not inventing one. The stuck moment is the "around the building" lot. First come is doing real work on a Saturday night on Swann. If those spaces are gone, valet in front is the plan, not a hunt down a residential side street that may not belong to the restaurant. South Tampa traffic on Swann is its own delay. 3601 is not Hyde Park Village. Do not follow navigation to 1501 and then walk it off. Those are different nights.

The food, without pretending the 2020 prices still exist

The 2020 prices for appetizers, entrées, and desserts are not something I am copying into a current note. Menus move, and quoting a six-year-old check as what olivia tampa costs now would be a lie. What matters is the shape.

One 2020 dinner there ran arancini with a liquid cheese center, wagyu carpaccio, a Neapolitan-style Margherita, bucatini carbonara, olive-oil-poached halibut, apple butter torta. A 2020 night, not today's menu.

Michelin names mini rigatoni with ragù Bolognese and herbed ricotta, and a pizza with truffle honey, speck, and scallion. In 2025 that pasta was still being talked about with a 24-hour Bolognese, along with the speck pizza on besciamella, and guests also mention yellowtail, squid ink pasta, lemon lobster pasta, and truffle parmesan fries. Pastas extruded and dried in house. I would order from the menu in the room. Confident categories: house pasta, pizza from the dough room, steak and seafood inside an Italian menu, cocktails, a sommelier. It can cook a steak. It is not a steakhouse, not a sushi counter, and not a Thai grill.

I did not have a current menu with dish names in hand from the restaurant itself, only the two addresses and the reservation details. That is why I am leaning on dishes I have seen named over the years, and why I am refusing to attach current prices.

What the room feels like, and where people jam up

The open plan looks polished, and some guests find the noise overwhelming. I believe that, because the dough room, the bar, the wine wall, and a full dining room are all supposed to be visible at once. The 2020 account is the same idea from the other direction: the room was busy enough that resetting an eight-top mattered more, for a few minutes, than seating a reserved pair. If you want a quiet conversation, say so when you book, and do not assume the lounge is quieter than the dining room just because it is called a lounge. Casual sophistication is the restaurant's phrase. Come-as-you-are is the family's phrase. I would still not read that as beachwear. It means the room is not a jacket-required club, and it also means a birthday table and a Tuesday pasta dinner are sharing one soundtrack.

Groups of seven and up have to call. Valet is in front; the free spaces around the building disappear. Saturday and Sunday mornings exist on the hours I was given and are easy to confuse with On Swann's brunch hours. Weekday lunch does not, on the hours I have. The St. Pete sister restaurant will happily take a reservation meant for Tampa if you pick the wrong city when booking. I was careful about that. Tampa and St. Pete are booked side by side with two phones. Pick 813-328-8866 and 3601 W. Swann if the night is Tampa.

The first week of reservations in 2019 reportedly booked in less than an hour. I cannot re-prove that rush, and I do not need to. The practical version in 2026 is simpler: this is a known South Tampa Italian restaurant with a Michelin selection, a visible pasta operation, and a party-size rule. Whether people say olivia tampa or olivia's tampa, ignore the apostrophe, ignore On Swann at 1501, ignore St. Pete unless you meant the beach city, and call if you are seven or more. Then look at the dough room while you wait, because that window is the point of the building.
