Notes on tampa beaches, and the beach that is not there

People ask about tampa beaches, tampa beach, beaches in tampa, beaches in tampa florida, beaches near tampa, and best beaches in tampa florida. I was looking for a named beach called Tampa Beach. I did not find one. The City of Tampa does not use that name. The city says it manages four beaches along the shores of Tampa Bay, open sunrise to sunset: Ben T. Davis, Picnic Island, Cypress Point, and Davis Islands. Those are bay beaches. They are not the Gulf strand people picture when they say tampa beach.

The geography I had wrong at first

Tampa sits on Tampa Bay and the Hillsborough River, not on a Gulf barrier island. The wide white sand is west, in Pinellas County, across the bridges. Bayshore Boulevard is the waterfront people walk downtown, and it is not a beach. I kept those three things separate once I understood them: four city bay beaches, the Bayshore seawall and sidewalk, and the Pinellas Gulf beaches a drive away. Calling all of that "Tampa Beach" is how visitors end up disappointed on a causeway.

The four the city actually names

Ben T. Davis Beach, 7740 W. Courtney Campbell Causeway, 33607. Metered parking, $2.50 an hour. This is the one I think fools people. It is on the Courtney Campbell, which is also the road Visit Florida names for Clearwater. You can see sand and water and think you have arrived at the Gulf. You are on a causeway beach on the bay, closer to the airport side of town than to Pier 60.

Cypress Point Park, 5620 W. Cypress Street, 33607. Free parking Monday through Friday until noon. Other times it is metered, $1 the first hour and $2 every hour after. The city has a contracted instructor for SUP and beach yoga Sundays 9 to 10 a.m.

Davis Islands. Here I got two different addresses, so I did not mash them into one spot. The city gives Davis Islands Seaplane Basin at 864 Severn Avenue, 33606, and does not describe the sand. Visit Tampa Bay gives Davis Islands Beach at 900 Martinique Avenue, Tampa, FL 33606, phone (813) 931-2121, and says anchored boats sometimes crowd the beach in front of this sandy spot near downtown. Their line is that it may not be the best for swimming but it is fine for reading or sunning. I am not sure they are the same patch of sand. Visit Tampa Bay already warns the Martinique spot may not be for swimming.

Picnic Island Park, 7409 Picnic Island Boulevard, 33616. The city does not give a parking price for Picnic Island. I did not invent one.

Hours for all four are sunrise to sunset. The city says Tampa's beach water is monitored by the Florida Department of Health under the Florida Healthy Beaches Program, and it tells you to check the latest tests before you swim at Ben T. Davis, Picnic Island, Cypress Point, or Davis Islands. That is the struggle on the city beaches. They are real, they are sandy, and the city itself is telling you the water is something you check, not something you assume. John Dellaquilla is the parks manager, 813-753-9417, office at 3402 W. Columbus Drive.

Bayshore is the walk, not the swim

I went to Bayshore because people point at it when they say the beach is in Tampa. The city calls it the Bayshore Linear Park Trail, address 312 Bayshore Boulevard, sunrise to sunset, phone (813) 274-8615. It is a 10-foot-wide sidewalk from the Columbus Statue Park at Platt Street to Gandy Boulevard. A three-mile on-road bike lane runs on the northbound lane between Rome Avenue and Gandy. Amenities the city names: benches, a water fountain, bicycle parking, a city marina, fitness stations. Parking at Ballast Point Park and Fred Ball Park. Fishing is not permitted along the trail. Coast Bike Share at Rome Street, and a Pirate Water Taxi stop near the Platt Street Bridge at the Gasparilla ship dock.

Hillsborough County describes the boulevard as looking over Hillsborough Bay, homes on the other side, with a 4.5-mile continuous sidewalk the county calls the longest in the country. The county mentions dolphins, manatees, rays, and herons. The seawall is flood protection, not a shore you swim from. The view is the trap. There is nowhere to put a towel.

The Gulf beaches people actually mean

Visit Florida says Clearwater Beach is about 25 miles west of Tampa and the drive usually takes about 30 to 40 minutes, depending on traffic, via FL-60 west across the Courtney Campbell Causeway. That is the same causeway as Ben T. Davis, which is why the two get confused. Clearwater Beach is in Pinellas County, west of Tampa and north of St. Petersburg, reached over the Clearwater Memorial Causeway. Visit Florida mentions Pier 60's nightly sunset, a 2018 number-one beach ranking, and Dr. Beach naming Caladesi in 2008. The "35 miles of sugar-sand" line is the Pinellas coast, not one beach. Sand Key is just south. Caladesi is a ferry from Honeymoon Island. Airports Visit Florida names: Tampa International and St. Pete-Clearwater.

Parking is the part I would not shrug off. The City of Clearwater says get there early. Pier 60's lot, 160 S. Gulfview Boulevard, has 141 spaces at $3.50 an hour, 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. On-street is $4.00 an hour, five-hour limit. Pay at a kiosk, ParkMobile, or text CLEARWATER to 727563. No RV parking on the beach streets, and nothing public north of the Acacia Roundabout. Private garages tell you to call for rates, so I did not copy one. Hotline (727) 562-4094, weekdays 8 to 4. The struggle is noon on a Saturday, not the hourly rate.

Indian Rocks Beach, also from Visit Florida: Pinellas County, on the Gulf, between Clearwater and St. Petersburg. They say it is approximately 27 miles west of Tampa and the drive typically takes about 40 to 50 minutes, depending on traffic. Shells, a seabird sanctuary you can walk to, a mangrove boardwalk, and a Mobi-mat onto the sand. I was not given a parking rate, so I am not inventing one. Visit Florida calls it laid-back. That is why I would send someone here instead of Pier 60. It is their adjective, not a ranking I measured.

Fort De Soto is the other one locals mean and visitors miss because the name is not Tampa. Pinellas County: 3500 Pinellas Bayway South, Tierra Verde. 1,136 acres, five keys, over 7 miles of waterfront including almost three miles of beach. Open 7 a.m. to sunset. Parking $6, plus $0.35 if you pay by app or text. Loggerheads nest April through September. More than 328 bird species. Attendance averages more than 2.7 million. Fishing piers need a license. Dogs on the sand only in the designated dog-beach area. Concessions are cashless. Visit Florida's directions: I-275 exit 17, west, left at the second light onto State Road 679. From the Gulf beaches, south through St. Pete Beach, left on the Pinellas Bayway, right on 679.

The figures for Fort De Soto disagree. The county says almost three miles of beach. Visit Florida says over six miles of beaches. The county says 236 campsites. Visit Florida says 238, all with water and electric. Dr. Beach number one in 2005 is something both mention. I would book through the county.

Nobody gave me a mile count from downtown Tampa to Fort De Soto or to St. Pete Beach, so I am not writing one. Visit Florida only uses St. Pete Beach as the turn in the directions. Indian Rocks is described as between Clearwater and St. Petersburg. That is as far as I will go.

What I would actually do

If someone in a Tampa hotel asks for the best beach in Tampa, I would say the city has four bay beaches and none of them is the postcard. Check the Healthy Beaches test if you stay on Ben T. Davis, Cypress Point, Picnic Island, or Davis Islands. If you want the Gulf, budget the Courtney Campbell to Clearwater and get there early, or keep going to Indian Rocks, or drop south to Fort De Soto and pay the $6. Bayshore is the sunset walk when you do not want sand in the car. There is no Tampa Beach among the city's beaches, and I stopped looking for one.
