People say james stadium tampa when they drop the first name. That means Raymond James Stadium, which gives its address as 4201 N. Dale Mabry Highway, Tampa, Florida 33607. This one is actually in Tampa. That already separates it from the Rays, who play at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg. I had both pins in mind, and they are not the same errand.

The stadium is blunt about who the building is for. It says the Tampa Bay Buccaneers became an NFL franchise in 1976, that they are in the South Division of the NFC, and that they were Super Bowl XXXVII champions for the 2002 season. It says the University of South Florida Bulls football program is a member of the American Athletic Conference. The stadium also hosts the ReliaQuest Bowl, the Gasparilla Bowl, Monster Jam, and a Breakaway Music Festival I did not look into past the name. The stadium's A-to-Z guide says the place debuted on September 20, 1998, and that it is named for Raymond James Financial, a firm headquartered in St. Petersburg, not for a person. The same guide says Raymond James is the official investment services and financial planning provider for the Buccaneers. So the dropped word is a company name, and the company is across the bay, and the stadium is on Dale Mabry. I can see why the name gets short.

The ticket office is where the stadium's own information starts to fight. The A-to-Z guide says it is in the South Plaza between the Suzuki Marine gate and the Fifth Third Bank gate, next to the stadium office, and open on event days only. Most of the event details put it in the same part of the building. The stadium's Steelers game details say the ticket office is in the South Plaza between Gates C and D. The lost-or-stolen instructions in the A-to-Z guide send you to the ticket office between Suzuki Marine and D. I do not know if D and Fifth Third are the same door. I know a visitor holding two official instructions can walk to the wrong plaza entrance. The stadium office number for normal hours is 813-350-6500, and on event days 813-350-6501. Buccaneers tickets are 813-879-2827. USF ticket operations are 813-974-3002, and ticket sales 1-800-GO-BULLS.

Mobile tickets are the rule, and the stadium keeps the mobile rules short on purpose. Tickets to all events are on a phone only. No screenshots. They want the ticket in Apple Wallet or Google Play before you reach the gate. They split the instructions into Buccaneers, USF, and all other stadium events through Ticketmaster. The A-to-Z guide adds that once you are in, each guest needs their own mobile ticket, and that you should turn the screen brightness all the way up. There will be no printed tickets. If the ticket will not load, that guide sends you to the ticket office between Suzuki Marine and Fifth Third.

The bag rule is a no-bag policy for every event. One clutch, no larger than 4.5 by 6.5 inches. Everything else goes to the car or to lockers the guide places on Tom McEwen Boulevard, south of Suzuki Marine. It calls the fee small and does not name it. Fans who refuse to be wanded are not admitted. Re-entry is only for a medical emergency, through a Tampa Sports Authority supervisor. Strollers and umbrellas stay out. It is open air, rain or shine. The guide advises against heels and sandals and requires a shirt. Footwear is advice. The shirt is a rule.

Kids are not one policy. The A-to-Z guide and the event details match each other, and they do not match across events. Buccaneers and the Gasparilla Bowl: every age needs a ticket. USF and the ReliaQuest Bowl: three and under free if they do not take a seat. Monster Jam and Supercross: two and under, same condition. The Karol G show was two and under. If you are bringing a toddler, the specific event's rule is the one that counts, not a memory of the last game.

Parking is cashless and contactless. The stadium's FAQ says all major credit cards, the price varies by event, and advance parking is a separate purchase. I am not writing a dollar figure. The A-to-Z guide says approximately 10,000 spots on site and approximately 15,000 off site. Buccaneers RVs and campers use Publix Lot 14 off MLK. Most other events use Lot 8 off Himes. Nothing stays overnight. Rideshare instructions disagree. The stadium's general getting-here directions say Uber and Lyft drop-off and pickup are both at Steinbrenner Field, and that concerts and heavy nights can move. The A-to-Z guide puts designated drop-off at the mass transit turnaround by the pedestrian bridge, calls Steinbrenner the main rideshare lot, and says pickup may be Steinbrenner for major events and mass transit for smaller ones. The October 3 USF game said mass transit. The October 4 Bucs game said Steinbrenner Field. Karol G said TBD. I would go by the specific event, not the general directions.

A few other disagreements I do not want to smooth over. Alcohol: two per person per purchase, none brought in, none taken out, 21 with ID. The stadium says sales on the main and upper concourses stop at the beginning of the fourth quarter, or the equivalent at other events, while the club may serve until the end. In the next breath it says, depending on the stand, sales stop at the end of the third quarter, start of the fourth. Same guide. The team store hours in the guide are Monday through Friday 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., Saturday noon to 5 p.m., Sunday closed, and then the October 4 event, which is a Sunday, has the team store opening at 9:30 a.m. The guide already warns that hours change with events. The parking rules still say advance parking is available for the Buccaneers, the Bulls, the Outback Bowl, and select others. Everywhere else the stadium says ReliaQuest Bowl. I am leaving the old name where I found it.

From the same guide, the pirate ship was built in 1998, owned by the Buccaneers, closed to the public during events, with cannons at Buccaneers games, and rentable on non-event days at 813-350-6500. The Hyundai West Club is described as 60,000 square feet with 178 4K monitors. The East Club is described as 64,000 square feet with Bar 76, named for 1976, and 76 beers. Club access varies by event. The guide lists 134 suites, seating 8 to 36, Super Bowls in 2001 and 2009, and a renovation begun in 2016 expected to exceed 180 million dollars. Tours run most Mondays through Thursdays and select Fridays at 2:00 p.m., reservations required. Private groups need at least 10 and two weeks' notice for a 10:00 a.m. tour. Pricing is a phone call, 813-350-6553, and they do not give a price up front. Wi-Fi is the network bucswifi. Any ticket resale is prohibited on the property, lots included. Concessions are cashless, with reverse ATMs at select spots. Guest services takes a text at 813-680-2827.

Tampa Sports Authority runs the stadium. The guide says the Authority is also the landlord for Benchmark International Arena and for Steinbrenner Field. That is the sentence that ties the mess together. The Lightning arena, the Rays' old hurricane home and the current Bucs rideshare lot, and this stadium are three different pieces of ground, and the rideshare instructions keep using Steinbrenner's name. A Lightning ticket, a Rays ticket, a Seminole Hard Rock hotel and casino ad, an AMC showtime, or a concert that is not on the stadium's schedule is not 4201 N. Dale Mabry. The concert that is on the schedule is Karol G on October 9, and even that one would not tell me where to send a car.

What I wrote at the bottom for myself: Raymond James Stadium, Tampa, Buccaneers and USF, plus the bowls and the shows the stadium actually has scheduled. Phone ticket, no bag bigger than a clutch, no cash at the lot, check the specific event for the gate time and the rideshare pin, and do not trust a single instruction about which door the ticket window is behind.
