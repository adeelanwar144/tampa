Notes on oxford exchange tampa, which is a daytime place and not a dinner

People say oxford exchange tampa, the oxford exchange tampa, the oxford exchange in tampa, and oxford exchange in tampa. Same building every time. 420 W. Kennedy Boulevard, Tampa, FL 33606. Phone (813) 253-0222. Email info@oxfordexchange.com. What I got wrong at first was the hour and the door. I had it filed with the restaurants, a place you book for dinner after the museum or after Hyde Park. It is not. They call the restaurant a daytime affair. Breakfast and lunch on weekdays, brunch on the weekend, afternoon tea. They say the restaurant serves until 5 p.m. The hours for the whole place are Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. If I want a table at 7, this is the wrong oxford.

The main entrance is not on Kennedy, even though Kennedy is the address everyone repeats. They say the main entrance is on West Grand Central Avenue. I thought that was the way people get stuck. You plug in 420 West Kennedy, the pin sits on a boulevard, and the door they want is around the corner on Grand Central. I would follow their directions, not the pin alone.

What is in the building

Blake Casper's line is that it started with a bookstore and an office. They gutted the inside, kept the outside, and made the restaurant, the Commerce Club, and the shop. The building went up in 1891, across from Henry B. Plant's Tampa Bay Hotel. From the 1920s an arcade of shops was in it. It sat vacant. Blake Casper and his sister Allison Adams first welcomed guests on September 24, 2012, with London clubs as the model and a bookstore as the way in.

The oxford exchange in tampa is a stack of rooms, not one cafe. The bookstore is what most people walk into first. New books, classics, signed first editions, children's books. They belong to the American Booksellers Association and the Southern Independent Booksellers Alliance. Bookstore extension 5, bookstore@oxfordexchange.com. A signed-first-edition subscription is the OE Bookshelf. Most of the photos people share are of the restaurant. The bookstore is why the building is there.

The restaurant is the daytime bistro. Art-filled dining room, open kitchen, or the Conservatory, vines and a retractable glass roof. Seasonal dishes, coffee, tea, cocktails. They quote Virginia Woolf, the line about dining well. Reservations are not required and they recommend them. Peak hours fill up. Call (813) 253-0222 extension 1, or book ahead directly. They do not have a waitlist, and they will not give you an estimated wait for a walk-in. Walk-ins sit if a table is free. For parties of 6 or more, the restaurant says email info@oxfordexchange.com. The largest party in the dining room is 12. Bigger than that is a private room upstairs through the events team, not a regular reservation. Catering to-go with free delivery inside 10 miles is extension 108, on a number they give as 813.252.0222, which is one digit off the main 253 line. I would dial the extension on whichever number I was actually given for catering, and I would not assume those two numbers are a typo I can "fix."

The Champagne Bar does not take reservations. Walk-up, limited seats, small plates. To-go is only there, only Monday through Friday, only in person. Not by phone and not on the weekend. A Saturday pastry is not a call-ahead.

The Coffee Bar is the other clock people mix up. Its own hours are Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and Saturday and Sunday 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. The building's hours say the weekend opens at 8:00. So on a Saturday the doors may be open at 8 and the coffee bar, by its own hours, at 9. I would not promise a latte at 8:15. Extension 113. They serve Buddy Brew. The signature drinks use a blend made for Oxford Exchange, and they say that blend is only sold at this coffee bar. Food is there too: specialty sandwiches, Wright's Gourmet pastries, and they mention avocado toast and a salmon bagel as OE classics. Beer and wine as well. No reservation.

TeBella is the tea partner, loose leaf, and you can buy the tea and the steepers at the bar inside. Traditional tea service on the weekday restaurant menu is $44, all day Monday through Friday, in the dining room or the Conservatory. Tower of sandwiches, scones, desserts, hot or iced TeBella. The monthly Afternoon Tea is different. First Sunday of the month, 3:00 to 5:00, in the Conservatory, bookings about 30 days out, $75, a larger tower. Children's tea is $55, almond butter and banana, grilled cheese, scones, two sweets, tea or juice. If I want the Sunday production I have to ticket it. If I want tea on a Wednesday I order it from the server after I sit. People book the $75 and arrive on the wrong Sunday, or they walk in on the first Sunday expecting the weekday tower. They split the two on purpose.

Upstairs is not more restaurant. Daytime events are in the Commerce Club. Meeting rooms book through the Commerce Club. The restaurant names a Smith Room as the upstairs private daytime space, and they point you at the events department rather than OpenTable. Nobody gave me a head count for that room, so I am not inventing one. The hard number they give is 12 in the dining room.

Rules I would not have guessed

There is no public wifi. Casper and Allison Adams wanted phones down. Wifi exists if you buy a Commerce Club day pass or you are a member. I thought that was the modern version of getting stuck. Someone sits down to work through lunch with a laptop and finds the network is not for guests. The building is pretty. It is not a coworking cafe unless you pay the club.

Pets: service dogs only. It is an enclosed building with a bookstore and food, and that is the line they draw.

Phones are fine. They lean into people photographing the room. A lighting kit is a different request, and they point professional shoots at a separate set of rules.

Outside dessert is a $40 fee. Wright's Gourmet House is exempt, and Wright's by the slice is already on the menu. Corkage is $25 a bottle. Gift cards work in the bookstore, the shop, the restaurant, both bars, and TeBella. They do not work at the Commerce Club, the Candle Pour, their e-shop, or Casa Cami.

Dress code is almost not a code. They quote Tom Ford, dressing well as manners, and then say it is intention rather than restriction, arrive as your best self. After Bern's and Meat Market, I noticed the lack of a prohibited list. I would still not treat that as a beach cover-up. The sentence is doing the work a bullet list does at the steakhouse.

Parking, which has more rules than the food

This is the section I would send someone before the menu.

City meters on West Grand Central, South Magnolia, South Cedar, South Hyde Park Avenue, South Plant, and South Parker. Pay in the ParkMobile app or at the walk-up machine directly outside the bookstore.

Attendant parking in the First Church of Christ, Scientist lot. Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Saturday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Sunday 1:00 p.m. to 4:00 p.m. No parking in that lot on Wednesdays from 5:00 to 9:00 p.m., which is after the restaurant is already closed, or on Sundays from 7:00 a.m. to noon. Sunday morning is the hole. Brunch is the busy meal, the building opens at 8, and the church lot is closed until 1. I would not plan Sunday brunch on that lot.

Weekend lot: Saturday and Sunday only, the Magnolia lot at 606 W. Kennedy Boulevard, free from 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Entrance on South Magnolia, in from West Grand Central. Free, but not at 8 a.m., and not on a weekday. Someone who heard "free parking at Oxford Exchange" and arrived Friday at noon does not have this lot.

So a Sunday at 10: the church lot is shut until 1, the free Magnolia lot is open from 9, meters are the other option, and the coffee bar's own hours may still say 9 even if the building says 8. That is three clocks before you have ordered.

I did not find a valet option. I did not find a garage they own. It is meters, the church attendant, and that one weekend lot. Kennedy is a real road. Grand Central is where the door is. I would leave the extra ten minutes.

How I would use it

The oxford exchange tampa is where I would go for breakfast, lunch, weekend brunch, or the tea, and for the bookstore on the way in or out. I would book if the table matters, extension 1, and I would not expect a waitlist quote. Six people, email. Twelve is the dining-room ceiling. Afternoon tea on the first Sunday is a ticket about a month ahead, $75, not the $44 weekday tower.

I would not put it on a dinner list. At 5:30 the day is over. Bern's, Meat Market, and the Columbia are a different plan. Oxford is the daylight meal, in an 1891 building, door on the side street, parking rules longer than the menu.
