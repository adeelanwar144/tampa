Notes on the riverwalk

People call it riverwalk, tampa riverwalk, tampa riverwalk tampa, riverwalk tampa, tampa riverwalk florida, tampa riverwalk tampa fl, riverwalk in tampa florida, riverwalk tampa fl, and riverwalk tampa florida. The doubled tampa is people who are not sure they have the right one. I wanted the city's own description. I also went through the city's Riverwalk public art program, the city's greenways FAQ, Friends of the Riverwalk's answers to common questions, and a 2017 walking guide from the Tampa Convention Center that warns it may be out of date. People also tack on hotels, Oldsmar, and an amusement park. I did not look into those, so they are not here. Fishing is, because it came up twice.

The city's featured-parks rundown for the Tampa Riverwalk does not give a length. It says its attractions list is partial. Four parks: Cotanchobee, Curtis Hixon Waterfront Park, Julian B. Lane Riverfront Park, Water Works Park. Museums: Glazer Children's Museum, Henry B. Plant Museum, Tampa Bay History Center, Tampa Museum of Art. Attractions: Benchmark International Arena, Armature Works, the Florida Aquarium, Sparkman's Wharf, the Straz, Sail Plaza, the Tampa Convention Center, Tampa Theatre. On the water, as names and not schedules: eBoats Tampa, Pirate Water Taxi, Riverwalk Boating Company, Tampa Bay Fun Boat, Tampa Bay Waterbike Company, Yacht Starship. Contact: 306 East Jackson Street, (813) 274-8211. No hours, no fee, no mile marker. I am not going to pretend the city settled the size there.

The length does not agree, and I got both figures

Friends of the Riverwalk answer "how long" with 2.6 miles. In one answer it runs from Armature Works to Sparkman Wharf. In another it begins at Beneficial Drive and Channelside Drive, is 2.6 miles, and extends just past Armature Works. Same length, opposite ends. They also say you can start wherever you like, which is the only line that matches how I would use it.

The city's public art program says "the 2.4 stretch" and later "2.4 miles." That is the city. Friends is not the parks department. I am not averaging 2.4 and 2.6. The city's featured-parks rundown, which should have refereed, does not give a number.

A third city figure is easy to misuse. The greenways FAQ, updated June 17, 2025, says the Riverwalk is part of the Ross J. Ferlita Greenways and Trails System, part of the Hillsborough River Greenway, and the spine of that system. The Hillsborough River Greenway is 14.17 miles. That is the greenway area, not the downtown path. Bayshore 5.02, McKay Bay 3.11, New Tampa 19.21, South Tampa 5.6, West Tampa 12.16. About 60 miles of public trails in the city, plans for about 125, concepts over 200. If someone tells you the riverwalk in tampa florida is 14 miles, that is the greenway bucket.

The convention center's December 7, 2017 guide also says 2.6 miles, then gives walks from the building: arena and Cotanchobee about 0.3, History Center about 0.4, Curtis Hixon about 0.6, the museums about 0.7, the Straz about 1.0, Water Works and Ulele about 1.7. The guide itself says it may no longer be accurate. I am leaving those distances in the 2017 column. I am not adding them up and calling the sum the length.

Hours are also two sentences

Friends say the path is open 24 hours and free. The city's greenways FAQ says trails in city parks are typically dawn until dusk unless posted. The Riverwalk is in that system and also a promenade with bars on it. I would look for a posted sign on the segment I was on. Sparkman Wharf, which I checked separately, wraps up at 9 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and 11 p.m. Friday and Saturday. The concrete and the businesses are not the same clock.

Bikes, and what the city will not rent

Bikes stay on designated paths. Gas-powered bikes, pocket bikes, mini bikes, go-karts, and motorcycles are not allowed. Electric wheelchairs, electric bicycles, and Segways are. Speed limit 15 mph. The city does not rent bikes at its parks. As of January 2025 it does not rent kayaks or paddleboards either, and tells you to find a private rental. The city names the boat companies and does not give their prices. I am not inventing a fare. The city also calls Blake Trail at Julian B. Lane one of its places to rollerblade. That is the city's wording about a trail, not a ranking I am extending.

Art, without using it to settle the miles

Andante, Heidi Lippman: glass panels, 200 linear feet under I-275, named for a walking tempo. Woven Waves, under the Fortune Taylor Bridge, formerly the Laurel Street Bridge: folded ceramic steel, patterns the city ties to West African, Cuban, and Scottish roots. America, America, Barbara Neijna, red steel from 1977, moved onto the path, two forms about 18 inches apart. Laura with Bun, Jaume Plensa, cast iron, 23 feet, more than 18,000 pounds, 2016, Tampa Museum of Art collection. Stay Curious, murals by Bask and Tes One on the Poe Parking Garage. Bronze busts, the city says, make up a Historical Monument Trail. The 2017 guide places monuments at Cotanchobee and a Henry B. Plant monument at Curtis Hixon. I would trust the city's art program for the sculptures and treat the 2017 placements as unchecked. That old guide also describes illuminated arches at night. The city's current rundown does not. I am not promising a light show off a guide that already doubts itself.

Rules that surprise people who think it is a park

Alcohol, per Friends: only in an approved specialty cup, only inside the cup zone, cups from authorized vendors. I do not have the map of that zone. I would not walk a regular bar cup toward the Straz and call it allowed. Fishing: Friends say no, not from the Riverwalk. People ask about fishing. The answer I found is a ban, not a pier.

Lost something: Friends say Parks and Recreation at (813) 274-8615. That is not the (813) 274-8211 the city gives in its attractions rundown. The greenways FAQ uses 274-8615 for trail maintenance and gives the parks office as 3402 W. Columbus Drive. I would use 274-8615 for a lost item or a broken stretch. I have not tested either phone. Paid filming needs a permit, Friends say. Events go through the city's special events office before you plan one.

Friends' own office, not the lost-and-found: 201 N Franklin St, Suite 2900, (813) 221-1539. Their 2025 tally is 1,078 volunteer hours, $808,928 raised, 405,525 program participants. Those are their numbers, not a Saturday crowd count. I am not turning the last one into how busy the path is.

What I would actually do with the disagreement

I would not tell anyone the Tampa Riverwalk is a single length. City art program, 2.4 miles. Friends, 2.6 miles, and they disagree with themselves about which end is the start. The 14.17-mile greenway figure is a different object. I would start at the end that matches the day. Channelside if the arena or the history center is the point. Curtis Hixon if the museums are the point. Water Works and Armature Works if the north end is the point. The city already says its list is partial, so a missing café is not proof the café is gone.

The practical failures are not the missing tenth of a mile. They are parking, which nobody here really solves, the dawn-to-dusk sentence sitting next to a 24-hour sentence, businesses that close while the path stays open, a drink rule that depends on a cup I have not seen, and a fishing ban for anyone who packed a rod because the word river was in the name.
