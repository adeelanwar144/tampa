Notes on oxford exchange tampa restaurant, which is the same building

People ask about the oxford exchange tampa restaurant or the oxford exchange restaurant tampa, and since I already had a note on the building, I wanted the dining room by itself. It is the same place. There is not a second Oxford, and there is not a dinner restaurant hiding under the bookstore. The address they give is still 420 W. Kennedy Boulevard, Tampa, FL 33606. Phone 813.253.0222. Email info@oxfordexchange.com. The main entrance is on West Grand Central Avenue, not on the Kennedy frontage the map pin likes. If someone sends me "the restaurant," I still walk to Grand Central.

What people get wrong about the restaurant

The mistake is the hour, not the map. They call the restaurant a daytime affair. Weekday breakfast and lunch, weekend brunch, afternoon tea. They describe fresh, simple, seasonal meals and name local and organic ingredients. They say to reserve directly or on OpenTable, and they say OpenTable counts them among the top 100 Best Brunch Restaurants in America. That is their claim, not a ranking I checked. It is why people think of it as brunch and not as a steakhouse.

This is the line I would text: all-day brunch and lunch at The Restaurant, served until 5pm. The hours for the building are Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 8:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. The doors can still be open after the dining room has stopped. A 5:15 table is the wrong request. A 7:30 dinner is a different restaurant. I had this filed next to steakhouse and Italian places because that is the company it keeps in Tampa food talk. The room is a bistro. They quote Virginia Woolf about the restaurant, and Esther Hicks about dining. Neither is a steakhouse voice.

Where they actually seat you

Two rooms, and they are not the coffee bar. An art-filled dining room with an open kitchen, or the Conservatory, vines and a retractable glass roof. The menus split into All day, Brunch, Beverages, Beer + Wine, and two Champagne Bar lists. I am only writing what I could make out on the food menus.

The all-day menu runs breakfast, small plates, salads, sandwiches, sides, dessert, and drinks. I saw a Wright's Griddled Muffin, avocado toast, burrata, a steak sandwich, fish tacos, Wright's cake by the slice, an affogato, a mimosa, a Bellini. Sandwiches come with fries, and there is an extra charge for a gluten free roll. The executive chef named on it is Josh Brouillette. At the bottom it says a 20 percent gratuity is added automatically for parties of 6 or more. Proteins are marked antibiotic and hormone free. A W means Wright's Gourmet House.

The brunch menu is not just a retitle. It adds a brunch tower, a cinnamon roll, a smoked salmon bagel, a Benedict, lemon poppyseed pancakes, steak and eggs, crab and avocado. The tower is mini frittatas, crispy fried chicken, bacon, sage sausage, a cinnamon roll, French toast, smoked salmon. Weekend is that menu. Weekday is the all-day menu. I am not copying every price. The tea prices below are the ones they give for tea service specifically.

Tea is two products, and the restaurant is only one of them

People book the wrong tea and then blame the dining room.

Traditional tea is $44. Monday through Friday, all day, in The Restaurant. Desserts, scones with clotted cream and preserves, tea sandwiches, hot or iced tea. You order it from the server after you sit, dining room or Conservatory. Their FAQ makes the same split. It is a menu item, not a ticket.

Monthly afternoon tea is the ticket. First Sunday of the month, 3:00 to 5:00, in the Conservatory, bookings about 30 days out. Afternoon tea is $75. Children's tea is $55, almond butter and banana, grilled cheese, scones, two sweets, tea or juice. Private tea in a private room is a third path, through an inquiry, aimed at showers. If I want Sunday tea I buy the ticket. If I want tea on a Wednesday I sit down and order the $44 service. Walking in on the first Sunday and asking for the weekday tower is how the afternoon gets ruined.

The all-day menu also has a traditional tea service with its own price on it. I am not going to reconcile that number with the $44 in this note. I would go by whichever menu the server is holding that day.

Reservations, parties, and the bars that are not the restaurant

Reservations are not required and they are recommended. Peak hours fill. Call 813.253.0222 extension 1, or book ahead directly. No waitlist. No estimated wait. You sit if a table is free.

Largest party in the dining room is 12. Bigger than that is a private room upstairs, through events, not a normal reservation. They state the 12-person ceiling plainly. Six is also the line where the menu adds 20 percent. I would email info@oxfordexchange.com before I assumed a room name for a bigger table.

The Champagne Bar does not take reservations. Walk-up, limited seats. They say small plates, and to-go orders only there, only Monday through Friday, only in person. Not by phone. Not on the weekend. The hours they give for the Champagne Bar are Monday through Thursday 10:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Friday and Saturday 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Sunday is given as 9:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m. I am not fixing that to 9:00 a.m. A bar that closes at 5 cannot open at 9 at night and still match the building. I would call before I promised a Sunday drink there. Champagne, rosé, cocktails, before the restaurant or while you are in the shop.

The Coffee Bar is a third clock. Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Extension 113. The building opens at 8 on the weekend. The coffee bar, by its own hours, opens at 9. They say Buddy Brew and a blend only sold at this bar, plus sandwiches, Wright's pastries, avocado toast, a salmon bagel. The coffee bar also describes itself as hand-roasted coffee, pastries, lite bites, beer, and wine. I am leaving both descriptions instead of picking a pastry case. No reservation there either.

To-go is the other trap. Weekday, in person, Champagne Bar. A Saturday call-ahead is not a thing they offer.

Rules that hit a table even if you only came to eat

No public wifi. Allison Adams and Blake Casper wanted phones down. Wifi is a Commerce Club day pass or a membership. A laptop lunch only works if I have paid for the club. Service dogs only.

They encourage phone photos. A lighting kit is a different request. Outside dessert is $40. Wright's Gourmet House is exempt, and Wright's by the slice is already on the menu. Corkage is $25 a bottle. Gift cards work in the bookstore, the shop, the restaurant, both bars, and TeBella. They do not work at the Commerce Club, the Candle Pour, their e-shop, or Casa Cami. Dress is intention, the Tom Ford line about manners, not a banned-item list. I would not read that as beach clothes.

Parking, because the table is useless if the car is wrong

Street meters on West Grand Central, South Magnolia, South Cedar, South Hyde Park, South Plant, and South Parker. ParkMobile, or the machine outside the bookstore.

Paid attendant lot at the First Church of Christ, Scientist. They say $12 for 2 hours. Monday through Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Saturday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Sunday 1:00 p.m. to 4:00 p.m. No parking there Wednesdays 5:00 to 9:00 p.m., which is after the restaurant has already stopped, or Sundays 7:00 a.m. to noon. Sunday brunch is the meal people want, and that lot is closed until 1.

Weekend lot only: Magnolia lot at 606 W. Kennedy Boulevard, free Saturday and Sunday 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Entrance on South Magnolia, in from Grand Central. Not a weekday lot. Not an 8 a.m. lot.

They also suggest Coast bike share on Grand Central, Uber, Lyft, and the Downtowner, plus the Fort Brooke Garage at 107 N. Franklin Street, about a 10 minute walk, hourly, entrance on Whiting. I do not have the garage's rates, so I am not inventing a dollar amount.

How I would use the restaurant

The oxford exchange tampa restaurant is the daytime dining room inside Oxford Exchange, same Kennedy address, door on Grand Central, served until 5. I would book extension 1 if the Conservatory or a weekend brunch table matters, and I would not expect a wait quote. Six people, I would email and I would expect the 20 percent on the check. Twelve is the dining-room ceiling. Tea on a weekday is $44 off the menu. Tea on the first Sunday is the $75 ticket. I would not put this room on a dinner list, and I would not send someone to the coffee bar at 8:15 on a Saturday and call it the restaurant.
