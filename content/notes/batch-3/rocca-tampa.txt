Notes on rocca tampa

Rocca Tampa is one restaurant, and the name is not ambiguous once you have the address. 323 West Palm Avenue, Tampa, Florida 33602, in Tampa Heights, phone +1 (813) 906-5445. It sits with The Pearl. It is not a downtown steakhouse, not a Hyde Park Italian, and not a second Rocca under a slightly different Tampa name. Rocca describes itself as Italian cuisine inspired by the old and the new, and it calls the place Michelin starred. Hours, per Rocca: Friday and Saturday 5 p.m. to 10 p.m., Sunday through Thursday 5 p.m. to 9 p.m. They repeat Friday–Saturday 5 to 10 and Sunday–Thursday 5 to 9 every time.

I am flagging a Monday problem because I do not want to paper over it. Rocca's public hours include Monday inside "Sunday through Thursday." But when they explain the Sunday-through-Thursday private dining minimum, they add a parenthetical "closed Monday." Those two statements do not say the same thing. Michelin has Monday 5 p.m. to 9:15 p.m., Tuesday through Thursday the same, Friday and Saturday 5 to 10, Sunday 5 to 9:15. So Michelin also shows a Monday service, and it runs the shorter nights 15 minutes past the 9 p.m. the restaurant gives. If I were booking a Monday, I would call or check the reservation book rather than trust any one of those three versions. I am not inventing a closure, and I am not inventing a 9:15 last seating. I am saying the three sets of hours I got do not match, and that is exactly where a reservation goes wrong.

What the restaurant is, and the star

Chef and owner Bryce Bonsack. Rocca's own story starts him in Monforte d'Alba, then handmade pasta and mozzarella pulled tableside. Italian recipes, New York technique. Crudo, bread, share plates, Italian wine, cocktails, then pasta or a dry-aged duck or wagyu.

He cooked at Blanca and Corton in New York, both starred. Rocca is his first as chef and owner. Rocca claims the first and only 10/10 from a local paper's restaurant critic, a local best-of vote's Best Chef in 2020 less than a year after debuting, and a Michelin star in 2023 and 2024. Rocca still says starred, present tense. I am not adding later years I could not confirm. Michelin has it at 323 W. Palm, Italian and contemporary, $$, date night, in-house pasta, spaghetti al limone with blue crab, a tableside mozzarella cart, and a chocolate custard with a pizzelle. No pets. Wheelchair access is noted.

The $$ from Michelin and the menu I saw do not feel like the same budget. I will get to the duck. People underbudget this place because a price symbol looks modest and the food is not priced like a neighborhood red-sauce room. It is Italian. It has a steak, the bistecca. It is not a classic American steakhouse, not a sushi bar, and not a Thai grill, whatever generic "Tampa restaurant" chatter tries to attach to it.

Names Rocca gives, if you are chasing who is actually in the building. Greg Proechel, COO of the group Tastes Pretty Good, back in Tampa as of August 2023, a level one sommelier. Dylan Ruhe, general manager. Michael Commins, chef de cuisine during the starred stretch and back to lead the kitchen, with time at Bresca and Jônt. Cole Biondi, assistant general manager. Kiley Wong-Li, private events.

Reservations, the 45-day book, and a short fuse

Parties over 6 email reservations@roccatampa.com. The book opens 45 days out. Grace period is 10 minutes, and the garage can eat that by itself. Rocca does not promise a hold past it. I would not walk up on a Saturday. Book inside the window.

Corkage, which Rocca states for regular dining and again for private dining: up to two 750 ml bottles, $50 per bottle. That is a stated fee, not a rumor. Two bottles is the cap they set, not a starting offer.

Parking, which is the actual headache

They do not offer valet. The recommendation is The Pearl garage at the east end of the building. Park in a retail spot. Do not park in a visitor spot. Visitor spaces are subject to towing. Then see someone on the Rocca team to validate, for up to 2 hours. I noticed how many ways that rule can go wrong, because it is all one rule and people follow half of it. East end, not whatever garage entrance the map drops first. Retail, not visitor. Validation is a conversation with the staff after you arrive, not a ticket machine you assume is automatic. Two hours is the validated window they name. A long tasting menu and a second bottle can push a night past two hours, and I would ask at the door what happens after that instead of discovering it at the garage exit. The towing warning comes from Rocca itself. I would not test a visitor space because it looked empty and closer to the elevator.

Private dining, with minimums I am not rounding

Two rooms, both with the same corkage rule.

Officina is a semi-private table inside the kitchen. They call it arguably the best seat. Maximum 8 guests. Minimum food and beverage spend $1,000. Reserve by calling 813-906-5445 or emailing reservations@roccatampa.com. This is the behind-the-scenes seat, not a quiet corner of the dining room. If someone in the party does not want to watch the kitchen, do not book Officina just because it sounds exclusive.

Cenetta is the private dining room. A family table, up to 16. A 70-inch TV, control of the music, a customized menu. Minimum spend $2,500 on Fridays and Saturdays, and $2,000 Sunday through Thursday, with that "closed Monday" parenthetical I already do not trust without a phone call. Inquiries go to events@roccatampa.com, not only the reservations inbox. I would not assume a Saturday Cenetta is available inside a normal 45-day window if you email the week before. The minimums are spends, not room fees I saw broken out separately. Ask what counts toward the minimum before you promise a host a number.

The menu I actually saw

Prices below are what the menu showed when I saw it, not a permanent card. Starters included wagyu carpaccio, salumi with prosciutto di Parma and gnocco fritto, a little gem with anchovy vinaigrette, and caviar service. Pasta: tagliolini al ragu $28, agnolotti $28, lobster cappellacci $55, egg-yolk tortello $26, spaghetti al limone with blue crab $39, wild-boar plin $27, gnocchi with trout roe $29.

Meats and fish: dry-aged Rohan duck, limited availability, at $155; pesce alla Ligure at market price; bistecca, Akaushi wagyu strip with salsa verde, at $125. The duck and the strip are the "we came for the star" orders, and they are also the orders that make a $$ tag feel misleading. Limited availability on the duck means it can be gone. I would not build a toast around it without asking when I book.

Cocktails were grouped at $21 stirred, $18 shaken, and $14 zero-proof, including a duck-fat Rohan old fashioned and a puttanesca martini. The wine list I saw was Italian-leaning: prosecco, Gavi, Langhe nebbiolo, Chianti, a Foradori Teroldego as a sommelier pick, plus Marsala, Moscato d'Asti, and Vin Santo on the sweet side. Vintages were given and will move, so I am not freezing them here.

Desserts on that menu: chocolate tart, riso al latte, pistachio gelato, semifreddo, basil custard, and a limited burnt cheesecake at $15. Michelin's chocolate custard with espresso cream and a pizzelle was not on the menu I saw. The menu moved, or Michelin's note is older. Tableside mozzarella is the bite both Rocca and Michelin talk about. Michelin says the flavor matters more than the cart. I would not skip it if they are pulling it.

Where people get stuck

The book opens 45 days out and closes, emotionally, about 10 minutes after your time. More than six people cannot just book themselves; they email. Monday hours do not match across Rocca's regular hours, its private dining parenthetical, and Michelin's 9:15. Parking is retail at The Pearl's east end, not visitor, not valet, validated up to two hours, towing if you guess wrong. Officina is eight seats and a $1,000 minimum inside the kitchen. Cenetta is sixteen and a four-figure minimum that jumps on Friday and Saturday. Corkage is $50 and two bottles. The duck is $155 when it is available and it is marked limited. A $$ symbol will not prepare anyone for that.

When people say rocca tampa, Palm Avenue in Tampa Heights is the restaurant. Call if the night is a Monday, email if the table is bigger than six, and park in the retail spot before you celebrate anything.
