The long name is the David A. Straz, Jr. Center for the Performing Arts, in downtown Tampa, Florida, and almost nobody says all of that out loud. I heard four shorter versions that all point at the same campus: the Straz, Straz Center, the Straz Center for the Performing Arts, and the old one, Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center, which people still crush into TBPAC. I went to the center's own audited statements so I would not treat the rename as a rumor. Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center, Inc. was incorporated on September 15, 1980, to run the facility under a lease with the City of Tampa. In November 2009 an endowment gift went to the foundation. For that gift, the center was renamed for branding, and it began doing business as the David A. Straz, Jr. Center for the Performing Arts effective November 11, 2009. The building did not move. The corporation did not dissolve. The name on the tickets changed, and the legal name on the statements did not fully let go of Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center.

That is the confusion I kept running into. People asking about the "straz performing arts center tampa florida" are often trying to confirm they have the right theater, because the old initials still circulate and because half the rooms inside have someone else's name on the door. The center's rental application lists the rooms with capacities, which is the practical version of the name problem. Carol Morsani Hall holds 2,610. Ferguson Hall holds 1,042. The Jaeb Theater holds 300. Shimberg Playhouse holds 100. TECO Theater is given as 100 to 200, which is already a range instead of a number, so I would not promise a planner a single headcount for that room. The center's own trivia adds a sixth stage, the Riverwalk Stage, and names the other four performance halls the same way once you get past the Jaeb: Morsani, Ferguson, Shimberg, TECO. If someone tells me they have tickets "at the Straz," I still have to ask which hall. Morsani is the Broadway-scale room. Ferguson is the next one down. The Jaeb and Shimberg are the small rooms, and the drop-off curb changes with the room.

The Jaeb name is a donor story, not a Straz story, and the center spells it out. Robert and Lorena Jaeb owned the Stop & Go convenience chain, about 450 stores, and sold it in 1985. The hall is named for them. Morsani is the name that used to be Festival Hall while the place was still being called the Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center. The center itself notes that in its interview with Bob Martinez. So even the main stage has had two names, and the campus has had two. Patel is a third layer. The center calls the school the Pallavi Patel Conservatory for the Performing Arts, built in 2004, for theater, dance, music, and voice. These days it just says Patel Conservatory. People say "the Straz" and mean a Broadway ticket, then find out the weekday traffic at the same address is kids in class.

Martinez is why the old name still feels like the real one to anyone who lived here. In the center's interview he is the mayor who ran in 1979 promising a downtown performing arts center, who pictured one hall and got several, and who ties the 1987 debut to the growth of the city. The center dates the public debut to 1987 and the original name to Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center. The interview dates the Straz rename to 2009, same year as the audit, without the November 11 day. I trust the audit for the day and the interview for the story. What I do not do is pretend David A. Straz, Jr. built the place in 1980. The audit says the rename was branding, in recognition of a donor, years after the doors first welcomed audiences. The man in the name is the endowment, not the mayor and not the architect.

The paperwork does not even hold one spelling. The audit's doing-business-as line is "David A. Straz, Jr. Center for the Performing Arts." The rental application shortens the corporation to "David A. Straz Center for the Performing Arts, Inc." and its address block says "David A. Straz Center for the Performing Arts, Inc," dropping the Jr. The tickets just say Straz Center. The logo is Straz Center. The annual report cover is "Straz Center for the Performing Arts." If I were addressing a check, I would copy the payee off the rental instructions, which say payable to the Straz Center for the Performing Arts, and I would not freelance the Jr. Mail for tickets still goes to 1010 N. WC MacInnes Place, Tampa, Florida 33602, and the phone people actually dial is 813-229-7827, which they also give as 813-229-STAR. The rental contact for someone trying to book the building, not a seat, is a different person: Jeanne Piazza at 813-222-1017. That application is explicit that it is not a contract, that you do not announce a date until a contract exists, and that they will only hold a date 30 days without a contract and deposit. I am noting it because "Straz Center" as a venue rental and "Straz Center" as a Broadway box office are two desks.

What people still call it shows up in the way the center talks about itself. The 2024-2025 report to the community is written as Straz Center, and the letter from the president still has to explain an expansion as "the new Straz." They broke ground on April 7, 2025, with Tampa Mayor Jane Castor, the board, DLR Group, and Creative Contractors. The report calls it an 80 million dollar expansion, to be completed in 2027. The letter describes a new outdoor stage, a rooftop bar, more dining, and a Patel Conservatory footprint 30 percent larger, with more classrooms and a dance studio. The campaign name is Boundless. So the vocabulary keeps stacking: TBPAC, then Straz, then the new Straz, then Boundless. I would not tell a visitor the theaters are shut for that work. When I looked, the center was selling tickets, not putting up a construction fence. It had The Sound of Music, Buena Vista Social Club, Black Violin, Misery, After Dark, and Hamilton, plus the 26/27 Bank of America Broadway season and Opera Tampa's 2027 season of three Italian works. The expansion is the next chapter. The current chapter is still a show.

The same report is where the name gets a size. The letter rounds to more than half a million guests and more than 1,500 performances. The graphics are tighter: 544,350 people, 1,541 performances, 40,613 complimentary tickets, and an estimated 132 million dollars in economic impact. Patel Conservatory that year counted 1,991 students and 447 scholarships worth 295,182 dollars. The letter rounded. The graphics did not.

The season rundown is how locals still narrate the place, by the show rather than by the donor. The Lion King came back. A Beautiful Noise, The Cher Show, MJ, Ain't Too Proud, the Grinch, Next Generation Ballet's Nutcracker, and a world premiere, The Boy Who Loved Batman. Jobsite is the resident company. Opera Tampa set Don Pasquale in Ybor City, did Candide, and closed with La Boheme. That mix is why "performing arts center" fits and "theater" is too small a word.

Parking is where the hall names stop being trivia. I am not reprinting every garage. The center tells you to navigate to the lot, not to "Straz Center," unless you are on valet. Drop-off is Fortune Street for the Patel Conservatory, Fortune Street or the Arrival Plaza for Morsani, the Arrival Plaza for Ferguson, and MacInnes Place at Tyler Street for the Jaeb or the Shimberg. If your friend still calls it TBPAC and your ticket says Ferguson, those are the same campus and different curbs. The Arrival Plaza is on Tyler Street and is a limited pad: tickets, meetings, valet, disabled spaces. It is not the main event garage. I learned that from the wording, not by missing a curtain. When I tried to sort out the rest of the visit details, I was simply told to call the ticket office. The name is famous. The visitor information was not having a good day.

I would answer "straz performing arts center tampa florida" like this. It is the downtown campus at 1010 N. WC MacInnes Place. It is the same place people still call TBPAC and the Tampa Bay Performing Arts Center. The Straz name dates from the November 11, 2009 branding change after an endowment, not from the 1987 debut. The rooms you actually sit in may be named Morsani, Ferguson, Jaeb, Shimberg, or TECO, and the school is Patel. The new construction is an 80 million dollar expansion aimed at 2027, under the Boundless campaign, and it is not a second Straz across town. Ask which hall is printed on the ticket before you trust a single map pin.
