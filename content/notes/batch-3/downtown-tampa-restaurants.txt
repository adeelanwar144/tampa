Notes on downtown tampa restaurants

People ask for downtown tampa restaurants, restaurants downtown tampa, tampa downtown restaurants, restaurants in downtown tampa, and best restaurants downtown tampa. The last one wants a ranking. I am not making one. I only wrote down places where I got the details from the restaurant itself. The Tampa Downtown Partnership's rundown of Water Street is a neighborhood directory, not a short list of tables; it mixes dining with hotels, a grocer, and services. Even with that in hand, you still have to pick the actual door. I did not chase Westshore. Ybor is where the original Columbia is, not the café below. Apartments kept coming up in the same breath, but not as anywhere to eat.

Columbia Café at the Tampa Bay History Center. The café puts itself on the Riverwalk in the downtown Water Street neighborhood, 801 Water St. The partnership directory has 801 Old Water St. and (813) 229-5511. I am not sure those are two different doors. Hours: 11 a.m. to 9 p.m. Sunday through Thursday, 11 a.m. to 10 p.m. Friday and Saturday. Lunch and dinner daily, a canopy-covered patio and bar, next to Benchmark International Arena. The menu is the Columbia group's Spanish and Cuban food. A few prices as I noted them, not as a promise: Spanish bean soup $10, the 1905 salad $18, the Cuban sandwich $15, paella $30. They have called themselves one of Florida's top restaurants since 1967. That is their slogan; I am not re-ranking it. They gave me nothing on parking.

The Sail and Big Ray's are part of the city's convention center property. Sail Plaza, 333 S Franklin St, called an urban oasis in Downtown Tampa. The Sail is a 360-degree waterfront bar: craft cocktails, frozen drinks, beer and wine, and a mojito they label Tampa Bay's Best. I am not taste-testing that label. Bar hours: Sunday to Thursday 11 a.m. to 11 p.m., Friday and Saturday 11 a.m. to 1 a.m. Dog friendly, all day. Big Ray's Fish Camp on the Riverwalk is the seafood side, and they call the grouper sandwich award-winning. Hours: Sunday to Thursday 11 a.m. to 10 p.m., Friday and Saturday 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. Live music Thursday night through Sunday afternoon, with the lineup announced on social media, so do not expect a printed schedule. This is the one spot that actually tells you how not to circle the block. Limited meter parking. Paid parking at the convention center garage, 141 E. Brorein St., or the Pam Iorio garage, 640 S. Florida Ave. Free TECO Line Streetcar, Dick Greco Plaza, stop 10. Pirate Water Taxi stop 7.

Harpoon Harry's Crab House, 225 S. Franklin St. They describe it as the heart of downtown, steps from the convention center and three blocks from the arena. Two-story room, a vintage Chevy truck bar, accordion doors, a second-story balcony. Seafood, sushi, USDA Prime steaks, and what they call comfort food. Hours: Sunday through Thursday 11 to 9, Friday and Saturday 11 to 11. Phone 813-321-4040. A crab boil they had announced was already marked postponed. I would not plan a night around an event that says postponed.

Malio's Prime Steakhouse, 400 N. Ashley Drive. In business here since 1969, with the family in the restaurant trade since 1945. A guest comment they share says it is nestled in downtown and you can sit outside near the water. The hours I could get: Monday and Tuesday 11:30 a.m. to 9:30 p.m., Wednesday and Thursday 11:30 a.m. to 10 p.m., Saturday 5 p.m. to 10:30 p.m. Friday and Sunday were left blank. I am not filling them in. Reservations at (813) 223-7746 or through OpenTable. They do catering and parties. Nothing on parking.

Cena, 1120 E. Kennedy Blvd, Suite 112, Grand Central, which they call the Channel District. That is downtown's south side, not the riverfront row. Modern Italian. They advertise a courtyard pizza bar and meatballs that beat Bobby Flay. That is their own boast, not a score I checked. Hours: Wednesday, Thursday, and Sunday 5 to 9, Friday and Saturday 5 to 10. Monday and Tuesday are not on their schedule, so I would not show up and argue. Reservations recommended, through their reservation system or at 813-374-8840. Their fees: corkage $25 a bottle, 20 percent gratuity for parties of 6 or more, $5 split fee, cake cutting $2.50 a person. Prices subject to change, in their words.

Anchor and Brine, 505 Water Street. Seafood-forward, waterfront, craft cocktails. Dining room: Sunday through Thursday 11 a.m. to 10 p.m., Friday and Saturday 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. The bar runs later, to midnight Sunday through Thursday and 1 a.m. Friday and Saturday. The restaurant gives its phone as 813-431-6951. The partnership directory has (813) 204-6388 for the same name and street. I got two different numbers and am not guessing which one rings. Their contact email is a Marriott address, so this is a hotel restaurant even when the sign does not shout it.

Lona is also at 505 Water Street, inside Tampa Marriott Water Street. Modern Mexican from chefs Richard Sandoval and Pablo Salas. Phone 813-204-6390, which matches the partnership directory. Cashless. I never got hours, so I am not inventing a dinner window. Parking is the detail I would screenshot. Complimentary valet up to 3 hours on non-event nights. On event nights, 50 percent off the daily rate, subject to availability. If the Marriott is full of a convention, the free valet is what disappears.

SiX, sixth floor of the JW Marriott Tampa Water Street, 510 Water Street. Elevated bistro, indoor-outdoor, bay and river views. Cashless. Valet, no rate given. Their hours do not stay still. One version says daily 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. with the kitchen until 10. Another says full service 11 to 10 and bar service 11 to 11. I would believe the kitchen closes before the bar, and I would not promise anyone a late entrée. Phone (813) 314-6666.

Boulon Brasserie and Bakery. They place themselves in the Water Street district of downtown, modern French classics, a bakery, email boulon@boulontampa.com. They did not give hours or a street number. The partnership directory puts them at 1001 Water St and (813) 768-9988. I am not borrowing hours from anyone other than the restaurant itself.

Ulele is the one I almost filed under downtown and then did not. 1810 North Highland Avenue, Tampa Heights, on the Riverwalk beside Water Works Park. Native-inspired food, a 10-foot barbacoa grill, on-site brewery, open since August 2014, Columbia Restaurant Group. Hours: Sunday through Thursday 11 to 10, Friday and Saturday 11 to 11. Reservations 813-999-4952. Groups, 813-310-3701. They say about 7,000 square feet, 240 seats inside, 95 in the beer garden, 50 on a rooftop terrace, lawn events up to 400. Men must wear sleeves in the dining room. If you walked north from Curtis Hixon because someone said downtown restaurants, you are now in Tampa Heights, and the sleeve rule is real. I count it as river-adjacent, not as a Franklin Street lunch.

What I kept tripping over

Downtown Tampa restaurants are three walks, not one block. Water Street, Channelside, and the convention center are one cluster if you are willing to use the Riverwalk, the streetcar, or the water taxi. Ashley Drive, where Malio's is, is the central grid. Kennedy in the Channel District, where Cena is, is a separate errand. Harbour Island is across the Garrison Channel. American Social describes itself as "just across the Garrison Channel from downtown," which is them telling you it is not this sidewalk. I am not filing Harbour Island here as if it were Franklin Street.

Parking is the real difficulty. The Sail was the only place that named garages and a streetcar stop. Lona's valet rule changes on event nights. SiX offers valet and does not give the price. Columbia Café, Harpoon Harry's, Malio's, and Cena gave me nothing on the car. A Lightning night or a convention will turn "limited meter parking" into no meter at all.

Hours do not follow one downtown clock. Cena is dinner only and skips Monday and Tuesday. Malio's Saturday starts at 5, and I will not invent Friday. Anchor and Brine's bar outlasts its dining room. SiX disagrees with itself by about an hour. Big Ray's closes earlier than The Sail on the same plaza.

Money rules hide in the small print. Lona and SiX are cashless. Cena adds gratuity at six people, plus corkage and a cake fee. I did not come across those rules at Columbia or Harpoon Harry's, which does not mean they do not exist. It means I am not repeating a fee I never saw.

I am not calling any of these the best restaurants in downtown Tampa. Anyone who says best or award-winning is selling a sandwich, a mojito, or a 1967 slogan. The useful way to sort them was geographic: Riverwalk patio, hotel floor, Channel District courtyard, or a steakhouse on Ashley. Pick the walk first.
