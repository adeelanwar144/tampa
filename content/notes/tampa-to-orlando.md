# Tampa to Orlando: My Drive Guide (I-4 Miles, Tolls, Bus, Train)

People ask it as Tampa to Orlando and Orlando to Tampa. The road is Interstate 4. The train people expect from Tampa is not running yet. The bus is real. I am not writing a door-to-door drive time, because nobody I trust publishes one that matches every driveway.

From downtown Tampa, I-4 was the ruler for Disney days and Orlando airport runs. The mileposts start at Malfunction Junction—not at TPA, not at a Brandon hotel, and not at Disney’s gate. That single fact cleaned up more bad GPS math for me than any app tip. **Last updated:** November 2, 2026.

## Trip Snapshot

- **Road:** I-4 east from the I-275 interchange (Malfunction Junction)  
- **Disney:** World Drive ~exit 62 (eastbound Disney traffic uses 62B toward the resort; 62A toward Celebration / World Drive south)  
- **Universal / north I-Drive:** exits 74–75 (Sand Lake, Universal Blvd, Kirkman)  
- **Downtown Orlando:** Colonial Drive ~exit 84A  
- **Tolls:** general-use I-4 is free; I-4 Express is optional, transponder-only, Orlando end only  
- **Bus:** FlixBus advertises about 1 hour 40 minutes, fares from the mid-teens  
- **Brightline Tampa:** still planning as of May 2026 city materials—not a ticket you can buy yet  

## I-4 as the Ruler

Per FDOT, I-4 is 132.30 miles, maintained by FDOT, signed east-west even though it runs diagonal, at least six lanes the whole way. It starts at the interchange with I-275 near downtown Tampa. That junction is Malfunction Junction, mile 0 for exit numbers. Mileposts measure distance from that interchange.

Going down the exit list using FDOT mileposts and the [I-4 exit number table](https://www.fdot.gov/traffic/trafficservices/exitnumb/i-4.shtm): World Drive, signed Disney World, is exit 62. Sand Lake Road, exit 74A, is mile 73.732, signed toward International Drive and Orlando International Airport. Orange Blossom Trail, exit 80, is mile 80.474. Colonial Drive, exit 84A, is mile 83.792, toward downtown Orlando. About 62 miles of interstate to the Disney World exit. About 74 to Sand Lake and International Drive. About 84 to Colonial Drive. If you start at Tampa International or in Clearwater, those miles are not the whole trip—add your own first leg.

East of the junction the road passes north of Ybor, then Brandon, Plant City, north of Lakeland, and the Polk Parkway. Weigh stations sit at mile 12. Rest areas are near Polk City and near Longwood, with restrooms, water, and vending.

For a shorter Gulf-side hop instead, see [Tampa to Clearwater](/guides/tampa-to-clearwater). For Naples south, use my [Tampa to Naples](/guides/tampa-to-naples) guide. Flying into Tampa first? [TPA](/getting-around/tampa-international-airport).

## Disney vs Universal vs Downtown Orlando Routes

These are different exits. Mixing them up is how you add a theme-park day of I-4 between gates.

### Walt Disney World (exit 62)

From Tampa, stay on I-4 east to **exit 62 / World Drive**. FDOT lists World Drive at exit 62. Recent eastbound work split the World Drive ramps: **62B** for northbound World Drive toward Walt Disney World Resort, **62A** for World Drive south toward Celebration. If your GPS still shows a single “World Drive” split after one ramp, confirm the live ramp letters before you commit—Disney-bound traffic wants the resort / northbound World Drive side.

You reach exit 62 **before** I-4 Express begins near mile 75. You can finish a Disney day without ever entering the toll lanes.

Nearby Disney-area exits on the same FDOT table: **64** (US 192), **65** (Osceola Parkway), **67** (SR 536 / Epcot Center Drive corridor), **68** (SR 535). Those matter if your hotel is on US 192 or near Disney Springs rather than on World Drive itself.

### Universal Orlando and north International Drive (exits 74–75)

Universal Orlando’s published directions: from **eastbound I-4**, take **exit 75A**, then left onto Universal Boulevard; from **westbound I-4**, use **exit 74B** and turn right on Hollywood Way. There are also approaches from Kirkman Road (**exit 75B**), Turkey Lake Road, and Vineland Road. Universal Boulevard connects the International Drive area to Universal via an overpass over I-4.

There is **no single I-4 exit signed only “International Drive.”** Practical approaches from FDOT’s table and local wayfinding:

- **74A** — SR 482 / Sand Lake Road (common for mid/south I-Drive)  
- **75A / 75B** — Universal Boulevard / Kirkman Road (north I-Drive and Universal)  
- **72** — SR 528 / Beachline (airport and east–west connector)  
- **71** — Central Florida Parkway (SeaWorld / south-of-Universal corridor)

I-4 Express starts around Kirkman (about mile 75)—right where Universal and north I-Drive traffic is densest. Disney traffic that stayed off at 62 never meets that merge. Universal and downtown traffic does.

### Downtown Orlando (exit 84A and neighbors)

For downtown, FDOT’s useful markers are **84A** Colonial Drive (SR 50) toward downtown, with nearby **80** Orange Blossom Trail, **82** SR 408 (East–West Expressway), and **83** South / Anderson Street depending on direction. That is roughly the mid-80s in exit numbers—past Disney, past Universal, into the city grid.

If your real destination is Orlando International Airport rather than downtown, Sand Lake / Beachline logic (74A / 72) is usually closer to the airport conversation than Colonial Drive. Confirm your hotel or terminal pin; “Orlando” is not one exit.

## Traffic and When Peak Pricing Shows Up

I am still not publishing a single drive-time promise. What I will publish is what I-4 Express and FDOT say about the Orlando end.

I-4 Express calls this the busiest interstate corridor in Central Florida. After the Express lanes debuted (February 26, 2022, following I-4 Ultimate on those 21 miles), reported stats for that Orlando section showed fewer crashes and higher speeds—those minutes are the **21 Orlando miles**, not the haul from Tampa. International Drive stays within about 1.5 miles of I-4 between Kissimmee and the Turnpike. The stretch that fights you is Disney through downtown, which is also where the toll lanes appear.

**Weekday peaks that matter for Express tolls:** I-4 Express and FDOT dynamic-tolling materials say drivers are most likely to see higher Express prices on the busiest segments near downtown Orlando during **6–9 a.m. and 3–7 p.m. weekdays**. Tolls adjust by segment (about every 15 minutes under dynamic tolling). General-use lanes stay free either way.

**Practical leave windows from Tampa (judgment, not a clock guarantee):**

- **Disney park open:** leave early enough that exit 62 is behind you before the Orlando commute peak eats the last third of the trip. Mid-morning after the 6–9 a.m. Express peak can be calmer at the Orlando end than aiming to arrive in that window—but park entry lines are a separate problem.  
- **Universal / I-Drive hotel check-in:** you will hit exits 74–75, which is Express territory. If you can avoid 3–7 p.m. weekdays for that last segment, do.  
- **Downtown Orlando evening:** Colonial Drive in the 3–7 p.m. weekday band is exactly when Express signs near downtown are most likely to spike.  
- **Weekends:** Express peak guidance is framed around weekday commute hours; weekend traffic still exists around parks—use live navigation.

Use live traffic and build buffer into park open, park close, and flight times. I will not invent a “always 90 minutes” number.

## I-4 Express Tolls (Orlando End Only)

The general-use lanes are not a toll road. I-4 Express is 21 miles in the median, roughly mile marker 75 just west of Kirkman Road to mile marker 95 east of State Road 436, in Orange and Seminole counties. Barrier-separated, speed limit 60, two axles and buses only. No large RVs or trailers.

Tolls move with traffic. I-4 Express says a segment can be as low as 50 cents. Overhead signs show the price before you enter; you never pay more than the sign you passed. If average travel speed in Express falls under 40 mph, statute drops you to the minimum Express toll. Transponder only: SunPass, E-PASS, E-ZPass, LeeWay, Peach Pass, NC Quick Pass, PIKEPASS, KTAG, TollTag, and other listed interoperable tags. No cash. No normal Toll-By-Plate. Enter without a tag and you risk a mailed bill plus fees / violation charges—confirm current amounts on [I-4 Express FAQs](https://i4express.com/faqs/).

**Rental cars are the trap.** An optional transponder means the rental company charges the tolls; enter without one—including by accident—and the company bills tolls and fees. Set GPS to avoid tolls unless you already took that product. Miss an express exit and you keep going. Road Rangers via *347 can help on the express section.

FDOT has studied more express lanes toward Tampa; those are proposals, not open pavement. Do not plan as if they exist yet. Check [FDOT](https://www.fdot.gov/) and live I-4 Express signage for rules.

### Bus detail (FlixBus)

FlixBus puts the Orlando–Tampa bus at 1 hour 40 minutes. They gave 82 miles one time and 83 another—both sit near the I-4 mileposts. Fares from $14.98, price moves. Seven trips a day. First Orlando departure 2:45 a.m., last 8:45 p.m. Orlando boards at 555 N. John Young Parkway and Florida Mall (1634 Florida Mall Avenue), no overnight parking. Tampa: 1315 N. Marion Street at the HART center, or USF on Holly Drive (campus parking rules). One carry-on to 7 kg and one checked bag to 20 kg. Wheelchair access if you note it when booking. Wi-Fi is offered subject to availability. Confirm current times and fares on FlixBus before you rely on them. Neither Tampa stop is a theme-park gate—budget a rideshare or local transit after you land.

### Train detail

Amtrak’s Floridian timetable includes a Fort Myers–Orlando thruway by chartered motorcoach. A clean run I could follow left Tampa Union Station at 9:25 a.m., Lakeland 10:10 a.m., Orlando 11:50 a.m., with a return Orlando 1:30 p.m. and Tampa 3:30 p.m. The long-distance Floridian train stops in both cities on a Chicago–Miami schedule—not an all-day shuttle. Confirm Amtrak for your date; times move.

Brightline is what people mean by skipping I-4. The City of Tampa’s transportation department, as of May 27, 2026, still marked the Orlando–Tampa extension as planning—about 85 miles from Orlando International along I-4, proposed stops at Disney or International Drive and Lakeland, Tampa station not chosen. Bond talk is not a train. Construction has not started. Do not buy a Tampa Brightline ticket. Miami–Orlando trains do not pick you up here.

## FAQ

### How many miles is Tampa to Orlando on I-4?

From Malfunction Junction (I-4/I-275), about 62 miles to the Disney World exit, about 74 to Sand Lake/International Drive, about 84 to Colonial Drive toward downtown Orlando—using FDOT mileposts along I-4.

### Which exit for Disney vs Universal?

Disney / World Drive is exit 62 (use the resort / northbound World Drive ramp—62B in the newer eastbound split). Universal from eastbound I-4 is typically exit 75A to Universal Boulevard; westbound often uses 74B to Hollywood Way. Confirm Universal’s current directions the day you drive.

### Are there tolls the whole way?

No. General-use I-4 lanes are not a toll road. I-4 Express is an optional toll facility near Orlando for eligible vehicles with a transponder.

### When are I-4 Express tolls highest?

I-4 Express says higher prices are most likely on busy downtown Orlando segments weekdays **6–9 a.m. and 3–7 p.m.** Read the overhead sign before you enter.

### How long does the drive take?

I do not publish a single drive time. Traffic varies. Use live navigation and build buffer into Disney, Universal, and airport days.

### Is there a train from Tampa to Orlando?

Not a frequent Brightline hop yet. Amtrak serves both cities on longer routes, and a thruway coach appears on some schedules. Confirm Amtrak for your date.

### Where does the bus drop in Tampa?

FlixBus lists the HART center on N. Marion Street and a USF stop. Neither is a theme-park gate.

## Final Thoughts

Treat Malfunction Junction as mile zero, get off at 62 for Disney before Express matters, use 74–75 only when Universal or I-Drive is the real destination, and do not let a rental-car toll product surprise you at the Orlando end. The bus is the simple no-drive option; Brightline to Tampa is still a plan.
