# Armature Works Tampa: Food Hall Guide (Not the Motor Shop)

“Armature Works Tampa” is the short name I actually say from a car—and the name that once led me to the wrong company. Tampa Armature Works is still a business name, and it is not the food hall. The hall borrowed the company’s name because the company once owned the building. Visitors mean 1910 N Ola Ave, Tampa, FL 33602, in Tampa Heights on the river—not a Riverview motor-repair headquarters.

From downtown, Armature Works is the easy upriver walk or short ride when everyone wants options without committing to one restaurant. **Last updated:** November 2, 2026.

## Visit Snapshot

- **Address:** 1910 N Ola Ave, Tampa, FL 33602 (Tampa Heights / Heights District)
- **Building hours:** Mon–Thu 7:30 a.m.–10 p.m.; Fri 7:30 a.m.–midnight; Sat 8 a.m.–midnight; Sun 9 a.m.–9 p.m.
- **Parking:** Four guest lots—most free Mon–Fri until 4 p.m. (excludes 220 lot); after 4 p.m. typically $10+ on non-event days
- **Venue fee:** 3% on checks (not a tip)
- **Not the same:** Tampa Armature Works / TAW motor company in Riverview

Confirm hours and parking on [Armature Works location](https://www.armatureworks.com/location/armature-works/).

## Why the Short Name Misleads

On April 12, 2022, Integrated Power Services announced it would acquire Tampa Armature Works, Inc. (TAW)—a Riverview-based, fourth-generation company founded in 1921 by James Arthur Turner, Sr., working electric motors, generators, transformers, and related equipment. Riverview is not 1910 North Ola. If a search for armature works tampa dumps you on tawinc.com or a motor shop, the words did exactly what they say and missed the hall.

A History Center curator’s version: Tampa Electric used the Oak and Ola barn for storage after streetcars stopped in 1946; Tampa Armature Works bought the building in May 1956. Oak and Ola’s own history (credited to Armature Works) dates the structure to 1910, says the streetcar system was abandoned in 1946, and says the company bought from TECO in 1960—not 1956. I keep both years. Either way, the machinery company left its name on the brick. The building visitors go to is the restored warehouse. Crane rails and the pit under the tracks are why the space feels industrial—not an invitation to a motor-repair tour.

## It Is Not One Restaurant

It sounds like one restaurant. It is a market plus several dining rooms. Heights Public Market is the counter side: beer and cocktails at Bar AW, smoked meat at Butcher n’ Barbeque, coffee at Buddy Brew, custard-style ice cream at Chill Bros, Venetian pasta to go at DalMoros, empanadas at Empamamas, pizza at The Fold, dumplings at Ling’s, and more stalls than one menu can hold. Sit-down rooms include Oak and Ola (Euro-American, wood-fired), Steelbach (Southern chophouse), Stones Throw (river pavilion), M.Bird (rooftop lounge), and Champagne Porch. Steelbach and Oak and Ola were both 2022 Michelin Recommended. Walking in and asking for “a reservation at Armature Works” is the mistake. Some counters get marked coming soon, so a name you remember may now be an empty slot.

Every check carries a 3% venue fee. The restaurant keeps it for facilities, equipment, and overhead—it is not a tip and is not distributed to staff. Tip separately. I thought people were struggling with the fee—and with the company-versus-hall mix-up—more than with finding the front door.

## Tampa Heights, Not Downtown or Ybor

The operator calls the hall the heart of the Heights District—about 50 acres anchored by the river and downtown—and puts the warehouse at about 73,000 square feet. The city places it in the Tampa Heights Riverfront CRA, charts about 73,440 square feet on a 2.8-acre parcel, year built 2019 on the chart, two-story market hall with co-working and events. Close to downtown is not downtown. The Pearl apartments—314 units on the block bounded by North Ola, West Palm, North Massachusetts, and West Ross—sit next door. Heading for the hall and ending up at a leasing office is the apartment version of the name mix-up. Ybor is a different historic district. A wooden Ybor streetcar barn burned in 1908; this brick barn is the one on Ola.

North Ola itself is a historic brick-paved street. Sidewalks on both sides; no marked bike lane on Ola (nearby lanes are on West Palm and North Highland). The Riverwalk stretch along the southern edge of this CRA, from Water Works Park to the Northern Avenue Bridge, finished in 2020 and sits directly across from the hall, with more than thirty-four slips for small boats. If your pin is a block off and you cannot see the water, you are not at the door yet. ZIP for the hall is 33602—one of the Tampa Heights ZIPs—not a forced swap to 33605 just because Ybor uses that code.

City project WTR-20-0011 will replace water mains along Ola in Tampa Heights, with construction planned for calendar year 2028—not a 2026 curb closure. Check again if you visit after that window starts.

## Parking: Free Until 4 p.m., Then Plan Ahead

There are four guest lots. All Armature Works lots except the 220 lot are free Monday through Friday until 4 p.m. Events and holidays vary. After 4 p.m. on non-event days, pricing typically starts around $10+. A garage about a block away sits in the same complex as Sprouts, F45, and Escapology. Meters on West 7th, West Palm, Oak, and North Highland run 8 a.m. to midnight, seven days a week, from $1.50 an hour, city-enforced.

Four o’clock on a weekday is when the free window ends—exactly when I would be arriving for dinner. If you care about free parking, get there early. The city has noted visitor spillover into residential streets north of West Ross; a permit idea for those streets had not been adopted when I checked. Do not treat a quiet residential curb as overflow just because the short name never mentioned parking.

Building hours (not every stall’s hours): Monday–Thursday 7:30 a.m.–10 p.m.; Friday 7:30 a.m.–midnight; Saturday 8 a.m.–midnight; Sunday 9 a.m.–9 p.m. Confirm each restaurant if you need a kitchen open late.

## Events Change the Day

When I checked in early October, there was a pumpkin patch for all of October. Fall Fest ran October 10–11 on the West Lawn and Market Street—free, with vendors and camel rides. Rides by the River happens every third Saturday—free, no ticket, cars lined up along the river. Private events use The Gathering, the Theatre, and Rooftop 220 (roughly 25 to 550 guests), plus private dining at Steelbach, Stones Throw, M.Bird, and Champagne Porch. A third Saturday means a car show, not a quiet hour at the market. The Heights District Fourth of July celebration for July 4, 2026, was listed as free admission 5–10 p.m. with fireworks—event parking prices vary.

For a quieter Riverwalk museum morning first, see [Tampa Museum of Art](/things-to-do/tampa-museum-of-art). For shopping west of downtown, [International Plaza](/shopping/international-plaza-tampa) is a different errand entirely.

## Tips From Our Visits

Keep “Armature Works Tampa” as the nickname, then pin the street: 1910 North Ola, 33602, Tampa Heights, hall on the river. Throw out Riverview and anything about rewinding a motor. Throw out the idea of a single host stand. Check the clock before trusting a free lot. Know the 3% line before you decide the server slipped in a tip. The name is popular because it is short—being short is why it points to two companies and one building with a dozen kitchens.

## FAQ

### Where is Armature Works?

1910 N Ola Ave, Tampa, FL 33602, in the Heights District / Tampa Heights on the Hillsborough River—not Ybor and not Riverview.

### Is parking free?

Most surface lots (excluding the 220 lot) are free Monday–Friday until 4 p.m. After 4 p.m. and on events/holidays, expect paid parking; the Heights garage near Sprouts is another option.

### Do I need a reservation?

For sit-down rooms (Oak and Ola, Steelbach, and others), book the specific restaurant. Market counters are walk-up. There is no single “Armature Works” reservation for the whole building.

### What is the 3% venue fee?

It is an operational fee retained by the restaurant for facilities and overhead. It is not a gratuity—tip staff separately.

### Is Tampa Armature Works the same place?

No. Tampa Armature Works (TAW) is the historic motor/power company whose name stayed on the brick. The visitor destination is the restored food hall and restaurants at 1910 N Ola.

## Final Thoughts

From downtown, this is the easy “everyone wants something different” dinner building—as long as you plan parking after 4 p.m. and tip on top of the venue fee. I would go back for a weekday lunch when the lots are free, and I would skip a third-Saturday car-show afternoon if I wanted a quiet market stroll.

Combine with [things to do in Tampa](/things-to-do/things-to-do-in-tampa) or a morning at the [Tampa Museum of Art](/things-to-do/tampa-museum-of-art). For a classic steakhouse night instead of a food hall, see [Rusty Pelican](/food-and-drink/rusty-pelican-tampa) on Rocky Point.
