# Tampa to St. Petersburg: Miles, Bridges, Express Lanes

People ask Tampa to St. Petersburg every which way—St. Pete to Tampa, with or without FL, with or without “to.” It is one question: how far on a road, and which road actually crosses the water. I am not writing a door-to-door drive time. Nobody official publishes one that matches every driveway.

**Last updated:** November 2, 2026 (FDOT mileage matrix dated 9/14/2026; Howard Frankland project notes checked against FDOT Tampa Bay materials).

## Quick Answers

- **FDOT city-to-city miles:** Tampa ↔ St. Petersburg **20** either way
- **Main busy crossing:** W. Howard Frankland Bridge (I-275) across Old Tampa Bay
- **Southern crossing:** Gandy Bridge (US 92)
- **Not this trip:** Courtney Campbell Causeway (SR 60) is Tampa–Clearwater
- **Express lanes on Howard Frankland:** electronic SunPass tolling as of Aug 22, 2026; passenger vehicles, vanpools, buses only
- **General-use lanes:** still available beside the express lanes

FDOT intercity mileage: [fdot.gov intercity highway mileage](https://www.fdot.gov/statistics/hwydata/intercity.shtm). Bridge project: [FDOT Howard Frankland project 422904-2-52-01](https://www.fdottampabay.com/project/417/422904-2-52-01).

## The Official 20 Miles

FDOT’s intercity highway mileage matrix (updated 9/14/2026) is the shortest distance between city pairs for mileage reimbursements. Tampa to St. Petersburg is **20**. St. Petersburg to Tampa is **20**. I am using 20 because both directions say 20—not because I averaged anything.

FDOT has no figure in that matrix for a neighborhood, a beach, the airport, or a hotel. Brandon is not one of FDOT’s cities. Wesley Chapel is not either. “How far from my Brandon hotel to downtown St. Pete?” is not answered by the 20, and I will not tack a guess onto it.

Nearby pairs stay separate:

- St. Petersburg ↔ Clearwater: 20 (Pinellas side—not a second copy of the bay crossing)
- St. Petersburg ↔ Sarasota: 37
- St. Petersburg ↔ Lakeland: 53
- St. Petersburg ↔ Orlando: 105 (Tampa ↔ Orlando is 85—do not subtract your way into a fake stop)

For the Orlando corridor instead, see [Tampa to Orlando](/guides/tampa-to-orlando). For Clearwater / Courtney Campbell, see [Tampa to Clearwater](/guides/tampa-to-clearwater).

## Three Bridges, One Trip Choice

Old Tampa Bay sits between the cities. Three county-to-county crossings matter in conversation; only two belong to Tampa–St. Pete.

### Howard Frankland Bridge (I-275)—the busy middle

The W. Howard Frankland Bridge is the central bridge from St. Petersburg to Tampa, carries I-275, and is by far the most traveled of the bay crossings. FDOT project 422904-2-52-01: new bridge work, limits from 4th Street in Pinellas to SR 60 in Hillsborough, **5.8 miles**, cities St. Petersburg and Tampa, construction cost about **$973.4 million**, started spring 2020, estimated completion late 2026, contractor Archer Western / Traylor Bros.

FDOT has opened new express lanes on I-275 across the new bridge—from 4th Street North in St. Petersburg to just north of the bridge in Tampa, in the median, separated by barrier walls and flexible plastic poles. **As of August 22, 2026**, tolls collect electronically 24/7 via SunPass. Only passenger vehicles, registered vanpools, and buses are allowed. FDOT’s fact sheet does not print a dollar amount on the project blurb; newsroom reports quoting FDOT put the opening toll around **50 cents** per trip—confirm live signage / SunPass before you budget. General-use lanes remain. Do not let a rental car drift into express lanes through a gap in plastic poles unless you meant to toll.

In 2025 FDOT put the new southbound I-275 bridge into service; the 1990s span converted to northbound; the 1960s bridge was removed. Posted speed on the bridge: **65 mph** effective August 22, 2026; **55 mph** on either side while approach work zones remain. A shared-use path—nearly seven miles, 12 feet wide—opened on the north side, sunrise to sunset, from the north end of 4th Street in St. Petersburg to the south end of Reo Street in Tampa. More connections (Ulmerton, additional 4th Street North, more at Reo) are separate projects still building.

The **5.8-mile** project length, the **nearly seven-mile** path, and the **20-mile** city pair are three different measurements. Do not merge them into “about six miles across.”

### Gandy Bridge (US 92)—the southern free-of-express-lane option

Gandy is the southernmost of the three bay crossings between the counties. It spans Old Tampa Bay from St. Petersburg to Tampa, carries US 92, maintained by FDOT. Bridge lengths: about 14,859 feet eastbound and 13,886 feet westbound—those are span lengths, not the city-pair trip. History: the 1924 bridge cut a much longer Tampa–St. Pete road distance; today’s official pair is 20, not a revival of old 19- or 43-mile storytelling. Eastbound span in use dates to 1975; westbound to 1997. The old Friendship Trail span’s demolition finished as of July 2016.

FDOT District Seven’s eastbound US 92 bridge repair over Old Tampa Bay (Hillsborough): work started November 3, 2025, about $2.9 million, estimated finish early 2027, repairs underneath not expected to affect Gandy Boulevard traffic; if a lane closure is needed, FDOT cited 8:30 a.m.–4 p.m. windows. The project files under Tampa even though the other end is St. Petersburg—do not read that as “Gandy is only a Tampa street job.”

### Courtney Campbell Causeway (SR 60)—wrong bridge for St. Pete

Northernmost crossing, Tampa–Clearwater. If a map offers it for St. Petersburg, that map sent you toward Clearwater’s side of the bay. Use the Clearwater guide instead.

## I-275 Identity Crisis North of the Bay

North of Ellenton, I-275 splits from I-75 to serve St. Petersburg / Pinellas via the Sunshine Skyway and Tampa via the Howard Frankland. Exit lists put I-275 north toward St. Petersburg around exit 228 from the I-75 system in that southern approach story; farther north, after I-75 bypasses the bay through Brandon, exit 274 at Wesley Chapel feeds I-275 south toward Tampa, St. Petersburg, and the airport. Same interstate number, different ends, different bridges. I-75 itself stays east past Brandon, the Selmon (exit 256), and I-4 (exit 261). Follow “I-275 toward St. Petersburg” without reading the exit and you can miss the bay or come in over the Skyway from the south.

Flying into [Tampa International Airport](/getting-around/tampa-international-airport) first? TPA sits on the SR 60 / western Tampa side—add your own first leg before the 20-mile city pair starts meaning anything.

## What the 20 Does Not Include

FDOT’s 20 is reimbursement distance between the two cities. It is not 4th Street to a specific downtown tower, not the airport, not the beach, and not the 5.8-mile bridge project length. Tropicana Field nights need the St. Petersburg end pinned—see [Tropicana Field](/events/tropicana-field)—not a vague “St. Pete” centroid. Downtown Tampa starts are not Westshore starts.

Night work windows appear on FDOT alerts (example logged in notes: overnight closures between the counties in early October 2026 windows). Check live FDOT Tampa Bay alerts before you assume a midnight bridge is quiet.

## Tips for Choosing a Crossing

- Default busy path: **I-275 Howard Frankland**; stay out of express lanes unless you have SunPass and meant to pay.
- Want to avoid express-lane geometry: consider **Gandy (US 92)** and live traffic.
- Heading to Clearwater beaches: leave this page and use Courtney Campbell guidance.
- From I-75 north: read whether you are being sent to Skyway–Pinellas or Howard Frankland–Tampa before the split decides for you.
- Pedestrians / cyclists: shared-use path sunrise–sunset on the new Howard Frankland north side—with incomplete connecting projects still underway.

## FAQ

### How far is Tampa to St. Petersburg?

FDOT’s official city-to-city figure is 20 miles each way (matrix updated 9/14/2026).

### Which bridge should I take?

I-275 / Howard Frankland is the central, busiest crossing. Gandy / US 92 is the southern alternative. Courtney Campbell is for Clearwater, not St. Pete.

### Are there tolls on Howard Frankland?

General-use lanes are not the express product. Express lanes toll electronically via SunPass as of August 22, 2026, for eligible vehicles. Confirm current toll amount on signage / SunPass.

### Is the Courtney Campbell the St. Pete bridge?

No. It connects Tampa and Clearwater on SR 60.

### Does FDOT publish drive times?

Not as a single official door-to-door promise for this pair. Use live navigation and build buffer for construction approach zones still posted at 55 mph.

## Final Thoughts

Quote **20** for the city pair, pick Howard Frankland or Gandy on purpose, and treat Courtney Campbell as someone else’s trip. Express lanes are optional toll capacity with vehicle limits—not the only way across. When the destination is Tropicana Field, Channelside, or a beach further into Pinellas, finish the pin after the bridge, not before.
