# Tampa Museum of Art: My Downtown Visit Guide

People shorten it every way: Tampa art museum, art museum Tampa, art museum in Tampa, art museum Tampa FL. They all mean the same building—the Tampa Museum of Art in the Cornelia Corbett Center at 120 W. Gasparilla Plaza. There is not a second, smaller “Tampa art museum” under another name.

I went on a weekday from downtown. The Riverwalk walk to Curtis Hixon was the easy part. The hard parts were parking, Thursday pricing, and telling this museum apart from Glazer Children’s Museum next door. **Last updated:** November 2, 2026.

## Visit Snapshot

- **Address:** 120 W. Gasparilla Plaza, Tampa, FL 33602 (Cornelia Corbett Center)
- **Hours:** Mon–Wed, Fri–Sun 10 a.m.–5 p.m.; Thursday 10 a.m.–8 p.m.
- **Adult admission:** $25 (advance purchase available)
- **Thursday 4–8 p.m.:** Art on the House, pay-as-you-will
- **Parking:** No museum-owned lot—City of Tampa / private lots nearby
- **Closed:** Gasparilla, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day; early close 3 p.m. on July 4, Dec. 24, Dec. 31

Confirm live hours and tickets on the museum’s [Plan Your Visit](https://tampamuseum.org/visit/) page.

## Parking Reality (No Museum Lot)

The museum does not own a parking lot. Everything nearby is City of Tampa or private. I used the William F. Poe Parking Garage and paid through ParkMobile zone 9913—or at the pay-by-plate station with the license plate entered before walking away. I watched people argue at the kiosk about how many hours to buy. Undershoot and you walk back. The museum also names the Royal Street Regional Lot (ParkMobile zone 9709) plus metered street spots. Gold-level Art+ patrons and above can get a Poe garage validation code at the front desk. I was not in that tier, so I paid.

ADA parking is fussier. Closest accessible spaces are on the 4th and 5th floors of the Poe garage at 802 N. Ashley Drive—take the southeast elevators so you do not cross the railroad tracks at W. Gasparilla Plaza. The museum counts 17 ADA spaces across five floors and a 7-foot garage clearance for most vans. The accessible museum entrance is the Vinik Family Education Center door on the east side, facing Glazer Children’s Museum. There is a drop-off curb on Gasparilla Plaza between the two buildings—it is not a parking space. I watched a rideshare sit there while the car behind got annoyed.

## Getting There Without a Car

The museum says it is the only museum in the country you can reach by boat, with two Riverwalk slips—first come, free. Pirate Water Taxi stop 12 drops at Curtis Hixon Park in front of the building. I did not come by boat. I did walk the river side, and the glass ground floor does what the renovation promised: you can see art from the path without buying a ticket.

HART routes 7 and 10 stop on W. Cass between the Poe garage and the library. Routes 1, 5, 19, and 30 stop on Tampa Street between Zack and Twiggs. TECO streetcar stop 11 at the Ft. Brooke garage is about a 15-minute walk—I would not sell that as “nearby” to anyone with tired kids. For a wider downtown day, pair this with the [Riverwalk](/things-to-do/tampa-riverwalk) or dinner near [Armature Works](/food-and-drink/armature-works-tampa) upriver in Tampa Heights.

## Hours, Tickets, and the Thursday Trap

Straight from the museum: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Thursday 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Closed Gasparilla, Thanksgiving, and Christmas Day. Early close at 3 p.m. on July 4, Christmas Eve, and New Year’s Eve.

Thursday from 4 to 8 p.m. is Art on the House—pay-as-you-will. That is the thing people miss. I heard someone at the desk ask why their phone still showed a $25 ticket on a Thursday evening. Posted adult price is $25. Adults 65+, Florida educators, professional artists, and military (when that discount is listed) are $15; K–12 students $5; children 6 and under free; college students with ID free; SNAP EBT free. Art+ members enter free. No refunds on purchased tickets. Do not prepay full price five minutes before a Thursday 4 p.m. arrival without knowing Art on the House exists.

Art on the Spectrum: last Sunday of the month the museum opens at 9 a.m. It is in the CARD autism-friendly directory, with staff trained with CARD USF, and they will hand you headphones, sunglasses, and sensory toys. Quieter stretches they name: Tuesday–Sunday 10–11 a.m., and Monday–Wednesday 10 a.m.–noon. Temperatures change room to room. There is a projection room marked for light sensitivity. Service dogs yes; emotional support animals no. Two public dog parks sit across Curtis Hixon if the dog needs a break. Complimentary wheelchairs at the welcome desk, first come. Galleries, classrooms, and restrooms are elevator-served, with portable chairs in the galleries.

Buy advance tickets through the museum shop when you want a locked date; discounted tickets need valid ID at the Welcome Desk.

## What Was on the Walls (Fall 2026)

When I went in early October 2026, the lead show was David Hockney: Perspective Should Be Reversed, from the Jordan D. Schnitzer collections—September 24, 2026 through February 28, 2027. Over 140 prints, collages, photographs, and iPad drawings across sixty years. People clustered at the iPad drawings and argued about whether a screen drawing belongs in a museum. In this show, it does.

Around it, permanent-collection rooms do more work than the Hockney banner suggests. Mysteries of the Nile: Ancient Egypt runs June 25, 2026 to June 25, 2028—the museum calls it the first Egypt show in Tampa. Eye of the Beholder (William Knight Zewadski Greek and Roman gift) through January 3, 2027. The Etruscans—about 70 objects, called the largest public Etruscan collection in the Southeast—through May 23, 2027. The Five Suns (Haiti’s Saint Soleil) from a collection the museum describes as one of the largest public holdings of Haitian modern art in the country. Avant Garde (women in the permanent collection, many with Tampa Bay roots) through April 4, 2027. Suchitra Mattai’s *to leave a trace* into May 2027. Sam Modder’s wall drawings *Her Hair Like the Sun and Clouds* July 23, 2026 to July 4, 2027—she teaches at the University of Tampa. Esterio Segura’s *Hybrid of a Chrysler* (winged 1953 Windsor) is back. If someone tells you this museum is only a small contemporary box, they walked the lobby and left. The antiquities and Caribbean holdings surprised me most.

The museum is in a centennial renovation and expansion for more exhibition and education space. I could see the finished clearer ground floor and art visible from Curtis Hixon. I did not see a finished new wing. People talk as if the museum is closed for construction. It was open—the construction story is the next phase, not a shutdown.

## Gallery Rules I Wish I Knew on the Sidewalk

Backpacks (including purse-style backpacks), bags over 11×15 inches, luggage, food, drinks, tripods, and umbrellas go in free lockers at the welcome desk. Personal photography is allowed in most permanent and temporary galleries unless a sign or visitor rep says otherwise—no flash, no selfie sticks, no tripods, monopods, stands, drones, or extra lights. Handheld cameras or phones under 3 pounds. Stop at the welcome desk and ask for the free digital companion guide (audio, exhibition stories, translation in 52 languages). Bring headphones. I did not, and the guide is useless if you are sharing one phone speaker in a quiet gallery.

Out-of-town perk I only found deep in the visitor info: Le Méridien downtown hands out Unlock Art passes that cover general admission for hotel guests. I was not staying there.

## Tips From My Visit

The mix-ups I watched at the desk and garage: people ask for “art museum Tampa FL,” then cannot tell this building from Glazer next door; they expect a museum lot; they prepay the wrong ParkMobile zone; they buy full price on a Thursday evening; they think military free is year-round when the museum only promises a seasonal window when it lists one; they think expansion means closed; they bring a backpack and get sent to a locker. None of that is hard once you know the visitor rules. Almost nobody learns them before they are already on Gasparilla Plaza.

## FAQ

### How much does Tampa Museum of Art cost?

Adult general admission is $25. Discounts include $15 for adults 65+, Florida educators, and professional artists; $5 for K–12 students; free for children 6 and under, college students with ID, SNAP EBT participants, and Art+ members. Thursday 4–8 p.m. is pay-as-you-will (Art on the House).

### Does the museum have its own parking?

No. Use nearby City of Tampa or private lots—commonly the Poe garage (ParkMobile 9913) or Royal Street Regional Lot (9709).

### What are the museum hours?

Most days 10 a.m.–5 p.m.; Thursday 10 a.m.–8 p.m. Last Sunday of the month opens at 9 a.m. for Art on the Spectrum.

### Is the museum closed for renovation?

No. It remains open during its centennial renovation and expansion. Confirm the day’s hours on the museum visit page before you go.

### Can I take photos?

Personal handheld photography is generally allowed in most galleries unless posted otherwise—no flash, sticks, tripods, or drones.

## Final Thoughts

From downtown, this is the art stop that actually sits on the Riverwalk—once you solve parking and Thursday pricing. I would go back for the antiquities and Caribbean rooms even if the banner show has rotated, and I would bring headphones next time for the companion guide.

Pair a museum morning with lunch upriver at [Armature Works](/food-and-drink/armature-works-tampa) or a broader [things to do in Tampa](/things-to-do/things-to-do-in-tampa) plan. For kids next door, Glazer is a separate ticket and a separate day job.
