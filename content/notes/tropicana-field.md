# Tropicana Field: Rays Ballpark Guide (St. Petersburg)

Tropicana Field is the Rays’ dome in St. Petersburg—not a Tampa street address. The club lists **1 Tropicana Dr, St. Petersburg, FL 33705**. People still type “tampa rays stadium” because the team name starts with Tampa and because 2025 home games really were across the bay at Steinbrenner Field. This page is about the building that is open now.

**Last updated:** November 2, 2026 (bag, gate, and parking rules checked against Rays ballpark pages).

## Visit Snapshot

- **Name:** Tropicana Field (historic names: Florida Suncoast Dome, ThunderDome)
- **Address:** 1 Tropicana Drive, St. Petersburg, FL 33705
- **Tenant:** Tampa Bay Rays (see [Tampa Bay Rays](/events/tampa-bay-rays))
- **Tickets:** MLB Ballpark app, mobile only
- **Gates:** Typically 90 minutes before first pitch weekdays; 2 hours weekends / Opening Day (confirm each listing)
- **Bags:** Clear-bag policy
- **Payments:** Cash-free
- **Phone (third-party listings often show):** 727-825-3137

Official visitor rules: [Tropicana Field guide](https://www.mlb.com/rays/ballpark/information/guide) and [security regulations](https://www.mlb.com/rays/ballpark/information/security).

## What the Building Is

The dome opened in the Suncoast Dome era (club materials date AECOM Hunt’s earlier work to 1990). The Rays (then Devil Rays) inaugural game was March 31, 1998. The Lightning once played here when it was branded ThunderDome, before moving downtown—useful trivia, wrong ticket today. Capacity and seating charts change with renovations; I am not inventing a seat count the club did not lock into my notes.

After Hurricanes Helene and Milton in fall 2024, the roof failed and the City of St. Petersburg funded a roughly $60 million repair ($59.7 million in one later figure). The last panel landed November 21, 2025. The club returned April 6, 2026. New turf, clay, warning-track mix, premium pockets, and a larger video board came with the reopen story. The Cownose Clubhouse—about a 35-foot, 10,000-gallon tank beyond right-center—houses stingrays that stayed through Milton. Statues returned at Gate 4.

A “New Ballpark” link appears in club navigation; usable design detail for visitors was thin when I checked. August–October 2026 reporting described Tampa-side financing votes aimed at a late-decade opening near Dale Mabry / Hillsborough College—still bond-and-build territory, not a second open gate. The Trop lease language in those accounts ran through 2028. For a game this season, you are still going to St. Petersburg.

## Entry, Screening, and Bags

Box office help at Gates 1 and 4 starts 90 minutes before first pitch for mobile-ticket problems. QR purchase codes sit at the windows. Children need a ticket from their second birthday. VIP Gate 4 is for suites and premium clubs (DEX Home Plate Club, The Baldwin Group Club, Two Rivers Home Plate Box, Chalk Box, Baseline Premier). Screening is Evolv walk-through. All gates are wheelchair accessible; elevators at Gates 1, 2, 4, and 6.

**Clear-bag rule (club language):**

- One single-compartment clear bag (plastic, vinyl, or soft PVC), roughly **12 × 12 × 6** inches (security pages also print **12 × 6 × 12**—measure to the posted inches either way)
- Plus one small non-clear clutch / wallet / fanny pack ≤ **5 × 7** inches
- Gallon-size clear storage bags are called out as acceptable on the security page
- Exceptions: medical-equipment bags; diaper/infant bags within posted size when a baby/toddler is present
- No storage for rejected bags

Hard-sided bags, backpacks, and oversized purses stay out. This is not Raymond James Stadium’s no-bag clutch rule—see [Tampa Buccaneers](/events/tampa-buccaneers) if you are mixing sports weekends.

## Food, Drink, and Cash

The ballpark is cash-free. Use cards or Rays Wallet. Reverse ATMs sit across from Gate 3 guest services and in the 3rd Base Food Hall; a Republic Bank ATM is in the Draft Room. Alcohol sales stop mid-eighth inning; 21+; two drinks per purchase; none carried in or out. Outside food is tightly limited: one sealed personal water (guest code: 20 oz or less) or one empty plastic bottle/cup per ticketed fan, with medical/childcare exceptions noted in the food policy.

First aid, presented with Orlando Health St. Petersburg, is between Gates 3 and 4 on the 100 level behind home plate, staffed with St. Petersburg Fire and Rescue. Protective netting extends home to foul poles at sections 137–138. Group sales: 727-825-3406. Seminole Hard Rock’s name appears in Rays group materials as **162 Landing** near the visiting bullpen—a group space, not a shuttle to Orient Road (see [Seminole Hard Rock Tampa](/guides/seminole-hard-rock-tampa) for the hotel itself).

MaintenX SkyDeck (Gate 6 elevator, above left field) is marketed as all-inclusive ballpark fare, beer, wine, and soft drinks. Confirm ticket inclusions the day you buy—older notes quoted a $49 starting point that may move.

## Parking and Rideshare

Rays lots are often prepaid. In postseason operations the club has said parking is **prepaid only**—not sold drive-up—and overflow sits in downtown St. Petersburg under city/private rules the Rays do not set. Lot 1 = premium. Lots 6 and 7 = general. ADA: Lots 1 and 7 (mostly 7), van-accessible off 3rd/4th Avenue South. Gate 4 drop-off faces Lot 1 on 16th Street South. Rideshare drop-off is commonly Gate 4; ADA may use Gate 1 or 4. A 2026 fan-guide note also splits rideshare: Gate 1 guests to Lot 8; ADA plus Gates 4 and 5 to Trop Drive on 16th—follow the night’s posted map when those conflict.

**Transit:** SunRunner stop on the north side (15-minute headways 6 a.m.–8 p.m.; 30-minute 8 p.m.–midnight). Looper baseball shuttle is mentioned for the 2026 season—verify stops before you rely on it. From Tampa hotels, budget the Howard Frankland / Gandy choice deliberately via [Tampa to St. Petersburg](/guides/tampa-to-st-petersburg).

## How This Building Differs From “Tampa Stadium” Talk

| If you meant… | Go here |
|---|---|
| Rays baseball (2026) | Tropicana Field, St. Petersburg |
| 2025 Rays temporary home | Steinbrenner Field, Tampa |
| Buccaneers / USF football | Raymond James Stadium, Dale Mabry |
| Lightning hockey | Benchmark International Arena, Channelside |
| Yankees Single-A Tarpons | Steinbrenner Field (see [Tampa Tarpons](/events/tampa-tarpons)) |

Tampa Sports Authority’s landlord role ties Raymond James, the Lightning arena, and Steinbrenner Field in civic paperwork—it does not merge the gates. Tropicana Field’s ownership story in club materials points at the City of St. Petersburg as ballpark owner—the civic landlord that had to restore a playable building after Milton. That ownership sentence is why “the city will fix the roof” and “the Rays play in Tampa Bay” can both be true without sharing a municipal limit line.

Older names still surface in search: Florida Suncoast Dome, ThunderDome, “the Trop.” They are historical labels for the same St. Petersburg footprint. A Lightning ticket from the ThunderDome era is nostalgia, not directions. A concert listing that only says “Tampa Bay” without Tropicana Drive is incomplete until you see the city.

## Game-Day Timeline That Usually Works

A practical St. Petersburg arrival from Tampa looks like this when parking is prepaid and gates follow the standard weekday clock:

1. Confirm first pitch and gate time on the ticket listing that morning.
2. Leave Tampa with bridge buffer—Howard Frankland express lanes toll electronically; general-use lanes remain available.
3. Park in your assigned Rays lot (or downtown overflow if that is the plan) and walk with the clear bag already packed.
4. Use Gate guidance on your ticket; VIP/premium holders should not join the wrong queue out of habit.
5. If you need cash converted, hit a reverse ATM before concession lines peak.
6. Alcohol cutoff mid-eighth means plan the last round earlier than a football fourth-quarter habit.

Weekend and Opening Day gates often open earlier (two hours in the standard fan-guide language). Postseason press notes sometimes publish custom gate and lot-open times—follow those over muscle memory.

## Accessibility Notes Worth Reading Twice

All gates are described as wheelchair accessible. ADA parking concentrates in Lots 1 and 7, with most spaces in Lot 7 off 3rd or 4th Avenue South; spaces are van-accessible. St. Petersburg police monitor placard use—if the placard holder is not in the vehicle when it parks, citations can follow. ADA rideshare can use Gate 1 or Gate 4 depending on the instructions in force. Elevators at Gates 1, 2, 4, and 6 matter if you are heading for upper levels or the SkyDeck elevator at Gate 6. First aid between Gates 3 and 4 is the on-site medical landmark behind home plate.

## Tips for First-Timers

- Put **33705** in the map, not a Tampa ZIP.
- Screenshotting tickets fails—use the Ballpark app live pass.
- Clear bag + tiny clutch; leave the backpack in the trunk.
- Buy parking before you leave Tampa when the club requires prepaid.
- Arrive early for postseason or promotional gates; batting-practice windows get crowded.
- Do not borrow Raymond James bag rules; the buildings disagree on purpose.
- Treat “New Ballpark” headlines as future planning, not tonight’s GPS destination.

## FAQ

### Is Tropicana Field in Tampa?

No. It is in St. Petersburg. The Rays’ branding says Tampa Bay; the curb says Tropicana Drive.

### What happened to the roof?

Hurricanes in 2024 damaged the roof; repairs finished with the last panel on November 21, 2025, and the club returned for 2026.

### Can I bring a backpack?

No, unless it qualifies under medical or diaper exceptions within posted rules. Use a clear bag.

### Where do I park?

Prefer prepaid Rays lots (Lot 1 premium; 6 and 7 general). Overflow is downtown St. Petersburg if club lots sell out.

### Is the stadium cashless?

Yes. Convert cash at reverse ATMs if needed before you hit concessions.

## Premium Clubs Without Guessing the Map

VIP Gate 4 is the premium door for suite holders and named clubs: DEX Home Plate Club, The Baldwin Group Club, Two Rivers Home Plate Box, Chalk Box, and Baseline Premier. If your ticket names one of those, do not follow the general-admission herd out of habit. SkyDeck access uses the Gate 6 elevator story in fan materials—another reason to read the ticket before you pick a curb drop. Group spaces such as 162 Landing (Seminole Hard Rock–branded in Rays group copy) are buyouts near the visiting bullpen, not a hotel shuttle.

Outside food and water rules are easy to oversimplify. One sealed personal water is the consistent idea; guest-code language also allows a 20-ounce sealed bottle or an empty plastic bottle/cup per ticketed fan. Medical and childcare exceptions exist in the food policy. When two official sentences disagree on wording, follow the stricter reading until guest services tells you otherwise.
